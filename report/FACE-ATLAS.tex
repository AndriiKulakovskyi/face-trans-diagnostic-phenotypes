{\sffamily\bfseries\color{faceaccent}\small ABSTRACT}\par\vspace{5pt}
{\small
Psychiatric diagnosis aggregates biologically and prognostically heterogeneous patients into single
categories. \textbf{FACE-ATLAS} reorganizes the harmonized three-cohort FACE baseline (bipolar disorder,
schizophrenia, major depression; $N=9{,}013$) around \emph{continuous, diagnosis-agnostic axes of variation},
and asks how far such a map can be pushed --- through five dependent questions: does it \emph{exist},
\emph{organize}, \emph{persist}, \emph{predict}, and \emph{guide treatment}?\par\vspace{4pt}
The map is estimated by a single global, missingness-aware \textbf{Bayesian sparse bifactor / ESEM} model with
\textbf{mixed likelihoods}, fit to each patient's \emph{observed cells only} (no imputation, full sample), with
diagnosis held out as \emph{metadata} and the axes discovered on the baseline visit. From ten candidate
constructs the data resolve a \textbf{nine-dimension map} --- a general functional-burden factor $G$ plus eight
weakly-correlated axes (cognition, metabolic, inflammatory, sleep, developmental-risk, suicidality, mania,
substance) --- with depression/anxiety instruments placed as \emph{cross-loading windows} and anhedonia
\emph{rejected}. The headline result: \textbf{biology --- metabolic and inflammatory load --- is the least
severity-entangled domain} (correlations with $G$ of $0.12$ and $0.07$, versus $0.39$ for cognition and $0.42$
for sleep), so biological risk rides largely on axes that overall clinical severity does not capture.\par\vspace{4pt}
On these coordinates the space is a graded \textbf{continuum, not discrete biotypes} (five structure-discovery
methods agree; adjusted Rand index $\approx 0$ against the DSM-5 subtypes), described by eight extreme
phenotypes with every patient a soft blend and severity as its spine. Scored forward onto the follow-up visits
the geometry \emph{persists} --- \emph{durable biology, moving symptoms}. It also \emph{predicts}, but modestly
and for \emph{functioning} only: the durable biological axes and the archetypes add \emph{group-level} signal
beyond diagnosis and severity (course-dependent; two-year functional-remission AUC $+0.017$ over
diagnosis$+$severity), not a large individual-decision gain. And on observational treatment-as-usual the map
does \emph{not} reliably \emph{moderate treatment} response --- lithium in bipolar disorder, the cleanest
contrast, is a well-powered \emph{null}.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Taken together}, FACE-ATLAS demonstrates a transdiagnostic biological map that is real, stable, and a
\emph{continuum} rather than a set of biotypes, carrying a small-but-genuine group-level prognostic signal for
functioning and \emph{no} demonstrable treatment moderation in observational data --- a deliberately
\emph{calibrated} account in which the modest and negative results are reported as such. Establishing
individual-level clinical utility or treatment guidance would require incident-event outcomes, randomized
treatment data, and external validation beyond this baseline observational cohort.}\par\vspace{7pt}
