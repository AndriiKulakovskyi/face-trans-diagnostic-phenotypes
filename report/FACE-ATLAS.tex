{\sffamily\bfseries\color{faceaccent}\small ABSTRACT}\par\vspace{5pt}
{\small
Psychiatric diagnosis aggregates heterogeneous patients into categories that are neither biologically nor
prognostically uniform. \textbf{FACE-ATLAS} reorganizes the harmonized, three-cohort FACE baseline
(bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, major depression; $N=9{,}013$) around \emph{continuous, diagnosis-agnostic
axes of variation} rather than the diagnostic labels themselves. The program is staged across four milestones
--- \emph{diagnostic cohorts $\to$ transdiagnostic dimensions $\to$ validated strata $\to$ prognosis/treatment}
--- of which this report documents the first: the \emph{measurement map}. The map is estimated by a single
global, missingness-aware \textbf{Bayesian sparse bifactor / ESEM} model with \textbf{mixed likelihoods} and
\textbf{soft loading priors}: clinical theory proposes a candidate ontology of ten dimensions, and the data
\emph{confirm, split, merge, downgrade, or reject} each. Three invariants are enforced throughout: estimation
uses each patient's \emph{observed cells only} (no imputation, full sample); diagnosis is treated as
\emph{metadata}, never an indicator; and dimensions are discovered on the baseline visit, with later visits
reserved for validation. A Gaussian-block marginalization with a low-rank Woodbury likelihood makes the
full-sample fit tractable without subsampling or imputation; non-Gaussian indicators enter through a hybrid
explicit/marginalized mixed-likelihood block. On the FACE data the model yields a \textbf{certified
nine-dimension map} --- a general functional-burden factor $G$ and eight weakly-correlated specific axes
(cognition, metabolic, inflammatory, sleep, developmental-risk, suicidality, mania, substance) --- with
depression/anxiety instruments placed as \emph{cross-loading windows} rather than a separate dimension, and
anhedonia \emph{rejected}. The map is hardened end-to-end (prior/estimator robustness, multi-seed
certification, resample robustness, cross-cohort invariance, and absolute-fit posterior-predictive checks
across both likelihood blocks) and projected to per-patient coordinates with reliability flags. The headline
finding: \textbf{biology --- metabolic and inflammatory load --- is the least severity-entangled domain}
($\varphi$ with $G$ of $0.14$ and $0.06$, versus $0.39$ cognition and $0.44$ sleep), so biological risk rides
on axes that clinical severity does not see. On these coordinates, \textbf{Milestone~2} asks whether patients
form discrete biotypes: a structure-discovery gate (five independent methods) finds none --- the
transdiagnostic space is a graded \textbf{continuum}, represented as eight extreme phenotypes with every
patient a soft blend, organized by biology and cognition far more than by diagnosis (adjusted Rand index
$\approx 0$ against the seven DSM-5 subtypes; coordinate variance explained $\approx 21\%$ vs $\approx 5\%$).
Severity is the continuum's spine, not a corner; metabolic and inflammatory load form their own extreme
phenotypes. \textbf{Milestone~3} then scores the fixed map and strata onto follow-up visits
(V0\,$\to$\,V1\,$\to$\,V2, never re-estimated) and asks whether the signal \emph{persists}: the measurement is
shown invariant across visits, and the M2 geometry replays over time --- the cohort slides along the severity
spine while individual biological positions and phenotype memberships are preserved (\emph{durable biology,
moving symptoms}), confirmed by a variance decomposition and an independent geometric test that agree.
\textbf{Milestone~4} then asks whether the map \emph{predicts}: on the fixed objects, an
errors-in-variables Bayesian model tests whether a baseline coordinate or stratum forecasts a two-year
outcome \emph{incrementally beyond diagnosis, severity, and the baseline outcome}. It does --- but
\emph{modestly}, and for \emph{functioning} only: the durable metabolic and inflammatory axes and the eight
archetypes add held-out predictive signal that survives attrition, measurement reliability and a
permutation null, is \emph{co-informative} with DSM-5 (complementary, not a replacement) and
\emph{course-dependent} (concentrated in the episodic bipolar and depression courses, saturated in chronic
schizophrenia), while severity itself is autoregression-determined. Crucially the increment is
\emph{group-level}: it sharpens a continuous functional forecast and separates archetypes, but the
individual-decision gain is small (two-year functional-remission AUC $+0.017$ over diagnosis$+$severity),
and the $14\%$--$60\%$ archetype-remission spread partly reflects baseline-severity differences between
archetypes rather than the incremental signal alone. \textbf{Milestone~5} then tests whether a stratum
\emph{moderates treatment}. Treatment data --- absent from the harmonized variables but recoverable from
the per-cohort source dictionaries, and harmonized here to common drug-class exposures --- is run through an
overlap gate, a propensity model and doubly-robust errors-in-variables moderation. On observational
treatment-as-usual the map does \emph{not} reliably moderate response: lithium response in bipolar disorder
(the best-identified contrast) is a well-powered \emph{null}, an antipsychotic metabolic/inflammatory signal
is suggestive but unconfirmed, and clozapine is confounded by channeling; the average treatment effects are
themselves fragile to unmeasured confounding. The prognostic signal does, however, \emph{survive}
adjustment for the drugs patients received. \textbf{Taken together}, the program demonstrates a
transdiagnostic biological map that is real, stable, and a graded \emph{continuum} rather than a set of
biotypes, carrying a small-but-genuine group-level prognostic signal for functioning and \emph{no}
demonstrable treatment-moderation in observational data --- a deliberately \emph{calibrated} account, with
the negative and modest results reported as such rather than as another over-claimed biotype. Establishing
individual-level clinical utility or treatment guidance would require incident-event outcomes, randomized
treatment data, and external validation that this baseline observational cohort cannot provide.}\par\vspace{7pt}
