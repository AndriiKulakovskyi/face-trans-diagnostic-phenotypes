\section{The measurement model}
\label{sec:model}

This chapter builds the measurement model from the bottom up. The promise is that, by the end, the sentence
\emph{``the likelihood of the Bayesian posterior is an ESEM measurement model marginalized over its latents''}
is something assembled from parts the reader fully owns, not taken on faith. We start from the candidate
ontology (\S\ref{sub:ontology}), recall what a likelihood actually is (\S\ref{sub:likelihood}), write the
generative model and the assumption that earns its factorization (\S\ref{sub:generative}), give each indicator
its own likelihood (\S\ref{sub:mixed}), integrate the Gaussian latents out into the covariance identity at the
heart of factor analysis (\S\ref{sub:marginal}), put the general factor under a microscope because the headline
scientific number lives there (\S\ref{sub:Gdepth}), confront the rotation problem that makes the raw model
non-identified (\S\ref{sub:identification}), and finally assemble the posterior, where the prior does a second
job beyond regularization (\S\ref{sub:posterior}). The evaluation of this likelihood at full sample size
(Woodbury) and the recovery of patient coordinates (scoring) are deferred to Chapters~\ref{sec:estimation}
and~\ref{sec:scoring}.

The composite depression/anxiety instruments (MADRS, QIDS, STAI) are deliberately \emph{not} a dimension: each

\subsection{What a likelihood is, and the object we build}
\label{sub:likelihood}
Everything reduces to Bayes' theorem, which is itself the product rule of probability used twice. With $\Theta$
the unknown structural parameters and $X_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the data,
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{p(\Theta\given X_{\mathrm{obs}})}_{\text{posterior}}\;\propto\;
\underbrace{p(X_{\mathrm{obs}}\given\Theta)}_{\text{likelihood}}\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{p(\Theta)}_{\text{prior}} .
\label{eq:skeleton}
\end{equation}
That single proportionality is the entire Bayesian skeleton. Everything else is two activities: writing the
likelihood (\S\ref{sub:generative}--\S\ref{sub:marginal}) and the prior (\S\ref{sub:posterior}), then computing
with the product (Chapter~\ref{sec:estimation}).

The crux is the word \emph{likelihood}. A probability model assigns a number to data given parameters,
$p(x\given\theta)$, and the same symbol reads two ways. Fix $\theta$ and let the data vary, and it is a
distribution \emph{over data} that integrates to one. Fix the data at what was actually observed and let
$\theta$ vary, and it becomes the \textbf{likelihood} $L(\Theta)=p(X_{\mathrm{obs}}\given\Theta)$: \emph{not} a
distribution over $\Theta$ (it does not integrate to one), but a \emph{score} answering ``how probable was the
data I saw, if this were the true $\Theta$?''

\begin{methodsbox}[The detective, not the dice]
Think of a detective at a fixed crime scene (the data, which cannot change). Running through suspects (values of
$\Theta$), each is asked: how plausibly would \emph{this} suspect have produced \emph{this exact} scene? The
likelihood is the plausibility score across suspects --- it need not sum to one over suspects, because suspects
are hypotheses ranked against fixed evidence, not a probability distribution. Our whole task is to write that
score for the ESEM model and then multiply it by the prior.
\end{methodsbox}

For this model the likelihood will turn out to be an ESEM measurement model \emph{marginalized over its latent
factor scores}. The next subsections construct it.

\subsection{The generative model and the assumption that earns it}
\label{sub:generative}
Behind the many measured quantities we posit a few unobserved \emph{causes} --- latent factors. The empirical
justification is parsimony: dozens of clinical variables move together, and the simplest explanation for
``everything correlates'' is a small set of shared drivers. Each patient $i$ has a general-factor score and a
vector of specific scores,
\begin{equation}
G_i \sim \Normal(0,1),\qquad D_i = (D_{i1},\dots,D_{iK})\Tn ,
\end{equation}
and we model each indicator as a \emph{linear reflection} of those drivers plus item-specific noise, keeping the
general factor as its own term:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta}
\eta_{ij} \;=\; \alpha_j \;+\; \lambda_{jG}\,G_i \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{jk}\,D_{ik}\;+\;\beta_j\Tn c_i .
\end{equation}
Read Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} as the recipe for the \emph{expected reading} of instrument $j$ in patient $i$: a
baseline level $\alpha_j$; a contribution $\lambda_{jG}G_i$ from overall burden; contributions $\sum_k
\lambda_{jk}D_{ik}$ from the specific factors (mostly the home factor, plus small cross-loadings); and an
adjustment $\beta_j\Tn c_i$ for nuisance covariates $c_i$ (age, sex, education, site, cohort, assessment year,
and --- later --- medication). Covariates adjust an indicator's \emph{mean}; they are never dimension
indicators --- like correcting a thermometer for altitude before reading the temperature, where altitude is not
a ``temperature factor.'' Keeping them out of the factor space is what prevents a cohort difference from
masquerading as a clinical dimension. The loading matrix $\Lambda\in\R^{J\times(K+1)}$ and the
factor-correlation matrix $\Phi$ are the scientific estimands. Stacking the items of one patient, with the
general column separated from the specific block,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xstack}
x_i = \mu_i + \underbrace{\lambda_G\,G_i}_{\text{general}} + \underbrace{\Lambda_D\,D_i}_{\text{specifics}} + \varepsilon_i,
\qquad \mu_i=\alpha+Bc_i,\qquad \Lambda=[\,\lambda_G\mid\Lambda_D\,].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Local independence --- the defining assumption.} To get one patient's likelihood we need
$p(x_i\given f_i)$, where $f_i=(G_i,D_i\Tn)\Tn$ is the full latent vector. Here we make the assumption on which
all latent-variable measurement rests: given the patient's true latent scores, the items are
\emph{conditionally independent},
\begin{equation}
p(x_i\given f_i,\Theta)\;=\;\prod_{j\in\Obs_i} p(x_{ij}\given f_i,\Theta).
\end{equation}
The idea is that the factors are the \emph{only} reason items correlate: knowing where a patient truly sits, the
leftover wiggle in item~A says nothing about the leftover wiggle in item~B. In the Gaussian linear model this is
\emph{identical} to ``the residual covariance $\Psi$ is diagonal'' --- local independence and diagonal $\Psi$
are two faces of one assumption, that the factors \emph{explain away} all shared covariance. (This is also why
filling missing cells is forbidden: the engine assumes the only thing linking items is the latent, so any
invented value injects covariance the model would mistake for a real factor.)

Because $f_i$ is unobserved and the likelihood must depend on $\Theta$ alone, we remove $f_i$ by the sum rule
(integrate over every possibility for it), then multiply across independent patients:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fulllik}
L(\Theta)=\prod_{i=1}^N p(x_i\given\Theta)
=\prod_{i=1}^N \int\Big[\prod_{j\in\Obs_i} p(x_{ij}\given f_i,\Theta)\Big]\,\Normal(f_i;0,\Phi)\,df_i .
\end{equation}
This is the complete, first-principles likelihood: a product across patients (independence of units), a product
across items (local independence), and an integral that removes the latent (the sum rule). Everything from here
is \emph{evaluating this integral}.

\paragraph{Bifactor / ESEM.} The structure in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} is a \textbf{bifactor / ESEM} (exploratory
structural-equation model)~\cite{reise2012bifactor,asparouhov2009esem,marsh2014esem}. \emph{Bifactor} means
every indicator may load on the general factor $G$ \emph{and} on its home specific factor; \emph{ESEM} means an
indicator is additionally \emph{allowed} a small cross-loading onto other factors, shrunk toward zero by the
soft priors (\S\ref{sub:roles}) --- unlike a classical confirmatory factor analysis, which forbids
cross-loadings outright and forces every instrument into exactly one box. The image to hold is a hub-and-spoke
(Figure~\ref{fig:bifactor}): $G$ is a hub wired faintly to every instrument; each specific factor is a spoke
wired strongly to its own pool; a depression/anxiety ``window'' is an instrument wired only to the hub.

\begin{definitionbox}[Two identifications of the specific factors]
\textbf{Bifactor (primary).} $D_{ik}\sim\Normal(0,1)$, $\Corop(D_k,D_l)=0$, and $G\perp D_k$ --- the cleanest
identification for a first stable fit; the specific block correlates only via $\Phi$, with $G$ held orthogonal.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Correlated-$G$ (sensitivity).} $D_i\sim\Normal(0,\Phi_D)$ in a variant that \emph{lets} $G$ correlate
with the biological specifics. This variant is essential: it tests whether ``$G\perp$ biology'' is an empirical
finding or merely an artefact of the bifactor constraint (\S\ref{sub:Gdepth}; Chapter~\ref{sec:results} shows
it refined the magnitude without changing the conclusion).
\end{definitionbox}

\subsection{Soft-prior loading roles}
\label{sub:roles}
How does theory enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta}? Through the prior on each loading. Every (indicator, dimension) cell
receives a prior \emph{role}; the default is shrinkage, not hard exclusion --- theory says where variables
\emph{should} load, and the likelihood is free to disagree (Figure~\ref{fig:priors}). Because a hybrid
soft-prior ESEM is safe to over-seed --- an indicator with no real signal shrinks toward zero --- every
construct-relevant usable variable is seeded as an indicator, and only genuine confounders are reserved as
covariates~\cite{carvalho2010horseshoe}.

\subsection{Mixed-likelihood observation model}
\label{sub:mixed}
The per-item factor $p(x_{ij}\given f_i,\Theta)$ uses the distribution appropriate to the variable's type, with
the linear predictor $\eta_{ij}$ entering through the relevant link. This is generalized-linear-model logic:
$\eta$ lives on an unbounded scale, and a link maps it to a probability, a rate, or a real value as needed,
\begin{equation}
X_{ij}\sim F_j(\eta_{ij},\theta_j),
\end{equation}
with $F_j$ chosen per indicator as in Table~\ref{tab:likelihoods}.

\emph{Use the right ruler for each quantity.} You weigh flour on a scale (continuous), count eggs as whole
numbers (count), and record ``is the oven on?'' as yes/no (binary). Forcing a count or a yes/no onto a
continuous ruler manufactures fake covariance --- an artefact of the rescaling --- that then biases the very
dimensions being estimated. The mixed-likelihood design keeps each item on its native scale and lets the
\emph{shared} prediction $\eta_{ij}$ be translated into the right kind of output, so binary suicidality and
continuous biology live in \emph{one} correlated factor space, related through $\Phi$, rather than being crushed
onto a pseudo-continuous scale or analysed separately.

\begin{definitionbox}[Observed-data likelihood (invariant I1, formalized)]
With $\Obs_i\subseteq\{1,\dots,J\}$ the indicators observed for patient $i$, the log-likelihood sums over
observed cells only:
\[
\log p(X_{\mathrm{obs}}\given\Theta)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\Obs_i}\log F_j\!\left(X_{ij}\given\eta_{ij},\theta_j\right).
\]
Missing cells contribute no term; a patient's latent coordinates are informed only by their observed
indicators, and there is no completed matrix at any point.
\end{definitionbox}

\subsection{Marginalizing the Gaussian latents: the covariance identity}
\label{sub:marginal}
For the Gaussian items the inner integral of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fulllik} has a closed form, and the result is the
identity at the heart of factor analysis. We derive it from the \emph{definition} of covariance, with the
general factor explicit so its special role is visible. Using $\E[G_i]=0$, $\Varop(G_i)=1$, $\E[D_i]=0$,
$\Covop(D_i)=\Phi_D$, $\E[\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_i\Tn]=\Psi$ (diagonal), and the two independence facts that
do the real work --- $\E[G_iD_i\Tn]=\mathbf{0}$ (the bifactor orthogonality) and $(G_i,D_i)\perp\varepsilon_i$
--- the cross-terms in $\Covop(x_i)=\E[(x_i-\mu_i)(x_i-\mu_i)\Tn]$ vanish and the survivors sum to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
\Sigma \;=\; \underbrace{\lambda_G\lambda_G\Tn}_{\text{general: rank one}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\Lambda_D\,\Phi_D\,\Lambda_D\Tn}_{\text{specific factors}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\Psi}_{\text{private noise}}
\;=\;\Lambda\,\Phi\,\Lambda\Tn+\Psi ,
\end{equation}
where the compact form uses $\Lambda=[\,\lambda_G\mid\Lambda_D\,]$ and the block-diagonal
$\Phi=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}\Tn\\\mathbf{0}&\Phi_D\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ --- the zero
off-diagonal block being precisely the encoding of $G\perp\text{specifics}$. Because $x_i$ is a linear
combination of jointly Gaussian quantities it is itself Gaussian, $x_i\sim\Normal(\mu_i,\Sigma)$.

\paragraph{The general factor's footprint.} $G$ contributes a \textbf{rank-one} term $\lambda_G\lambda_G\Tn$:
every pair of items $j,j'$ co-varies through $G$ by exactly $\lambda_{jG}\lambda_{j'G}$. A single shared source
touching all items produces a rank-one covariance pattern. The analogy: $G$ is a background hum every microphone
picks up; because they all carry the \emph{same} hum it correlates every pair of channels, and because it is one
source that correlation pattern has rank one. The specific factors are extra instruments heard only in their own
clusters of microphones ($\Lambda_D\Phi_D\Lambda_D\Tn$); the residual $\Psi$ is each microphone's private static.

\paragraph{Missing data is just the marginal property.} For patient $i$ with observed set $\Obs_i$, a foundational
property of the multivariate normal is that any marginal is obtained by \emph{deleting} the unobserved
coordinates from $\mu$ and $\Sigma$:
\begin{equation}
x_{i,\Obs_i}\sim\Normal\!\left(\mu_{i,\Obs_i},\;\Sigma_{\Obs_i\Obs_i}\right).
\end{equation}
``Integrating out the missing cells'' is therefore not an extra procedure --- it is the marginal property of the
Gaussian. You grade each student only on the exams they actually sat; a blank neither helps nor hurts and is
never invented. Summing $\log\Normal(\cdot)$ over each patient's observed sub-vector is \emph{exactly} the
full-information maximum-likelihood (FIML) objective~\cite{enders2010missing} --- the precise identity
exploited, and turned into a tractable computation by the Woodbury identity, in Chapter~\ref{sec:estimation}.
Here we need only the fact that the model implies Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma}.

\subsection{The general factor $G$ in depth}
\label{sub:Gdepth}
The general factor is what makes this a \emph{bifactor} model rather than an ordinary correlated-factors model,
and its (non-)entanglement with the specifics is the central scientific question --- so it is worth a microscope.
Three facts distinguish $G$: unit variance (which fixes its scale), orthogonality to every specific
($\E[G_iD_i\Tn]=\mathbf{0}$, the defining bifactor constraint), and independence from the residual. The middle
fact lets $G$ be ``how much overall'' while the specifics carry ``what kind,'' the two not contaminating each
other. $G$ is further \emph{anchored}: only functioning and global-severity items are allowed to define it, and
those anchor items have their \emph{specific} loadings pinned near zero (the \code{g_anchor_on_specific} prior
of Table~\ref{tab:priorroles}). Without such anchoring a column of nearly equal loadings could be absorbed into
$G$ or redistributed among the specifics, and the two would be confounded.

\paragraph{The headline lives in the off-diagonal block of $\Phi$.} Recall the term that vanished in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma} by orthogonality, $\E[G_iD_i\Tn]=\mathbf{0}$. The correlated-$G$ sensitivity model is
exactly the model that \emph{unzips} that zero, freeing the off-diagonal block $\phi_{Gd}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:corrG}
\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}1 & \phi_{Gd}\Tn\\ \phi_{Gd} & \Phi_D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Sigma=\lambda_G\lambda_G\Tn+\underbrace{\lambda_G\phi_{Gd}\Tn\Lambda_D\Tn+\Lambda_D\phi_{Gd}\lambda_G\Tn}_{\text{the terms orthogonality had removed}}+\Lambda_D\Phi_D\Lambda_D\Tn+\Psi .
\end{equation}

\begin{keyresult}[The very term the clean derivation dropped is the one the analysis exists to estimate]
The vector $\phi_{Gd}$ --- the correlations between $G$ and each specific factor --- is the headline of the
entire enterprise. ``Biology is the least severity-entangled domain'' is, mathematically, the statement that the
$G$-row of $\Phi$ is near zero for the metabolic and inflammatory entries (values around $0.12$ and $0.07$) and
far larger for cognition and sleep (around $0.39$ and $0.42$; Chapter~\ref{sec:results}). The bifactor's
orthogonality is the \emph{null}; the correlated-$G$ arm measures how far from it the data sit. Two related
lenses agree on the ordering --- the bifactor direct loading $|\lambda_{jG}|$ and the correlation $\phi_{Gd}$ ---
and reporting both is what turns ``biology is separable'' from an artefact of one constraint into a finding.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Identification and the rotation problem}
\label{sub:identification}
One foundational fact explains \emph{why a prior is even needed}. A parameter is \textbf{identifiable} if
distinct values always produce distinct distributions over the observable data. For Gaussian data the observable
distribution is captured entirely by $(\mu,\Sigma)$, and the factor model has a disease: take any invertible
$M$ and rotate,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rotation}
\Lambda^\star=\Lambda M,\quad \Phi^\star=M^{-1}\Phi M^{-\top},\quad f^\star=M^{-1}f
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\Lambda^\star\Phi^\star(\Lambda^\star)\Tn+\Psi=\Lambda\Phi\Lambda\Tn+\Psi=\Sigma .
\end{equation}
The rotated parameters imply the \emph{identical} $\Sigma$, hence the identical likelihood, and are
indistinguishable by any amount of data --- two recipes that always yield an identical-tasting dish. The loadings
are \textbf{not identified} on their own. Confirmatory factor analysis resolves this by fixing enough loadings to
exactly zero that no nontrivial $M$ preserves the structure; the Bayesian ESEM instead resolves it with the
prior (\S\ref{sub:posterior}), which is the more flexible route --- it allows cross-loadings but breaks the
symmetry softly~\cite{asparouhov2009esem,marsh2014esem}.

\subsection{Assembling the posterior, and the prior's second job}
\label{sub:posterior}
We now have both ingredients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:skeleton}: the likelihood is the ESEM model of
\S\ref{sub:generative}--\S\ref{sub:marginal}; the prior encodes theory. Beyond the loading roles
(Table~\ref{tab:priorroles}), the remaining priors are weakly informative: intercepts
$\alpha_j\sim\Normal(0,1.5)$; residual scales $\sigma_j=0.05+\mathrm{HalfNormal}(1)$ (the $+0.05$ floor guards
against a Heywood case --- an item whose noise collapses to zero, claiming a factor explains it perfectly);
ordinal cutpoints ordered-normal; count dispersion $\phi_j\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$; and the factor correlation
$\Phi\sim\LKJdist(\eta{=}2)$, whose density $\propto\det(\Phi)$ puts more mass on weakly-correlated matrices ---
a gentle thumb toward near-orthogonal factors, without forbidding correlations~\cite{lewandowski2009lkj}.

The first job of the prior is ordinary regularization. The second is the one \S\ref{sub:identification} set up.
The likelihood is unchanged by the rotation $\Lambda\to\Lambda M$, but the \textbf{prior is not}
rotation-invariant: a rotation scrambles the loadings, so an entry the prior wanted near zero is no longer near
zero and $p(\Lambda)$ drops. Therefore the posterior
\begin{equation}
p(\Lambda\given X)\;\propto\;\underbrace{p(X\given\Lambda)}_{\text{rotation-invariant}}\;\underbrace{p(\Lambda)}_{\text{not invariant}}
\end{equation}
concentrates at the \emph{one} orientation where the small loadings align with the soft-zero priors and the home
loadings sit positive and substantial; the positive truncation additionally kills the sign-flip ambiguity.

\begin{keyresult}[The shrinkage priors are the identification device, not decoration]
The likelihood alone leaves a continuum of equally good loading matrices (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rotation}); the prior
\emph{selects} the interpretable one. A corollary supplies the natural robustness check: under a flat loading
prior the posterior mode equals the maximum-likelihood (FIML) solution, so a prior-free refit landing on the
same loadings shows the structure is \emph{earned from the data, not manufactured by the priors}
(Chapter~\ref{sec:validation} reports Tucker $\varphi=1.00$).
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{The prior atlas: the theory half of the deliverable}
\label{sub:prioratlas}
The soft-prior loading matrix is itself an artifact --- the \emph{prior atlas} (Figure~\ref{fig:prioratlas},
Appendix~\ref{app:prioratlas}), an indicator$\times$factor heatmap of where each instrument is \emph{expected}
to load, generated from configuration alone with no patient data. It encodes a block-diagonal primary structure
(each factor anchored by its own pool), a faint $G$ column over every specific item (the bifactor mechanism by
