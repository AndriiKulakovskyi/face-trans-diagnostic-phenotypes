\section{Acceptance gates and sampler diagnostics}
\label{app:gates}
A stage is \certified\ only if it passes all gates; otherwise it is \provisional.
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small\sffamily
\caption{The acceptance gate and the diagnostics it reads.}
\begin{tabular}{L{3.4cm} L{4.0cm} L{6.6cm}}
\toprule
\hdr{diagnostic} & \hdr{gate} & \hdr{what it detects}\\
\midrule
\rowcolor{facebg} $\widehat{R}$ (R-hat) & $\le 1.01$ & between- vs.\ within-chain disagreement (non-mixing)\\
ESS (bulk \& tail) & $\ge 400$ & effective independent draws after autocorrelation\\
\rowcolor{facebg} divergences & $=0$ & Hamiltonian integration failing on pathological geometry\\
Heywood & $\max_j|\lambda_j|$ capped & loadings explaining too much / residual collapse\\
\rowcolor{facebg} PPC (Bayesian SRMR) & not grossly violated & absolute fit: model-implied vs.\ observed correlations\\
WAIC & lower preferred & out-of-sample predictive fit for model comparison\\
\rowcolor{facebg} Tucker $\varphi$ & $\ge 0.95$ invariant & loading-pattern congruence (robustness / invariance)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The per-stage gate above certifies the continuous stages at full $N$. The joint mixed-likelihood fit, whose
explicit-latent block mixes more slowly, is certified at the \emph{largest $N$ that mixes} ($\widehat{R}\le
1.04$, $\mathrm{ESS}\ge 112$, $0$ divergences, $\mathrm{BFMI}\ge 0.41$) with a cross-seed resample-stability
guard (Tucker $\varphi$ $0.993$), per §3.6/§4.5 of the methods of record.

\section{Plain-language statistical glossary}
\label{app:statglossary}
A non-specialist guide to the statistical machinery, each entry giving the intuition rather than the formula.
{\small

\textbf{Modelling.}
\emph{Bayesian inference / posterior} --- instead of a single best-fit number, a probability distribution over
the values consistent with the data, so every estimate carries its own uncertainty.
\emph{Bifactor / ESEM} --- a factor model in which one general factor touches every item while each specific
factor touches its own cluster; ESEM additionally lets an item carry small cross-loadings onto other factors
(a classical confirmatory analysis forbids them).
\emph{Soft prior} --- a starting expectation for where an item should load that the data are free to override:
``theory proposes, the data dispose.''
\emph{Mixed likelihood} --- using the probability model appropriate to each item's type (continuous, binary,
count, ordinal) rather than forcing everything onto one scale.
\emph{Observed-data likelihood / FIML (full-information maximum likelihood)} --- fitting from each patient's
\emph{filled-in} cells only; blank cells contribute nothing and are never imputed.\\[3pt]

\textbf{Computation.}
\emph{Marginalization} --- averaging over a hidden variable instead of estimating it one case at a time; here
it removes the per-patient latent scores from the sampler.
\emph{Woodbury identity} --- a matrix-algebra shortcut that inverts a ``diagonal $+$ a few shared directions''
matrix cheaply by working in the small factor space, which is what lets the model run on all patients at once.
\emph{R-hat ($\widehat{R}$)} --- a convergence check: independent sampler runs are compared, and values near
$1.00$ mean they agree (the sampler mixed).
\emph{ESS (effective sample size)} --- how many genuinely independent draws the autocorrelated sampler is
worth.\\[3pt]

\textbf{Fit \& validation.}
\emph{ELPD / WAIC} --- measures of out-of-sample predictive accuracy (how well the model predicts data it did
not see); higher ELPD, or lower WAIC, is better.
\emph{Posterior-predictive check (PPC) / SRMR} --- simulating fresh data from the fitted model and asking
whether it resembles the real data; an SRMR below $0.08$ is the usual ``good fit'' line.
\emph{Tucker congruence ($\varphi$)} --- the cosine similarity of two loading patterns: $1.00$ identical,
$\ge 0.95$ ``the same factor.''
\emph{Measurement invariance} --- whether an instrument means the same thing across groups (cohorts, visits),
so that a change in score reflects the patient, not the ruler.\\[3pt]

\textbf{Change \& stratification.}
\emph{Errors-in-variables / regression dilution} --- correcting for the fact that a predictor is measured with
error; ignoring it flattens the estimated effect toward zero, worst where the measurement is noisiest.
\emph{ICC (intraclass correlation)} --- the share of a variable's variation that is stable between patients
(\emph{trait}) versus fluctuating within a patient over time (\emph{state}).
\emph{Archetypal analysis / Extreme Deconvolution} --- describing each patient as a blend of a few ``pure
extremes'' (corners of the cloud); Extreme Deconvolution first subtracts each patient's measurement noise so
the blend reflects what they \emph{are}.
\emph{Adjusted Rand index (ARI)} --- agreement between two groupings, scaled so $0$ is chance and $1$ is
identical.\\[3pt]

\textbf{Causal inference.}
\emph{Propensity score} --- a patient's estimated probability of receiving a treatment given their baseline
profile, used to compare like with like.
\emph{Overlap / common support} --- whether treated and untreated patients are comparable enough to estimate
an effect at all.
\emph{IPW / IPTW (inverse-probability-of-treatment weighting)} --- re-weighting patients so the treated and
untreated groups look alike on measured baseline factors.
\emph{Doubly-robust estimation} --- pairing a treatment model with an outcome model so the effect estimate
stays unbiased if \emph{either} one is correct.
\emph{E-value} --- how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to explain a result away; a small
E-value means a fragile result.}
