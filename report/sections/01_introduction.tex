\section{Introduction and scientific framing}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem: heterogeneity inside the diagnostic label}
Bipolar disorder (BP), schizophrenia (SZ), and major depressive disorder (DR) are defined by polythetic,
consensus criteria: a patient qualifies by meeting some threshold number of symptoms drawn from a longer
list. Two patients with the \emph{same} diagnosis can therefore share almost no symptoms, while a given
patient's biology, cognition, and course can resemble someone with a \emph{different} diagnosis more than a
same-diagnosis peer. For prognosis and treatment selection this categorical resolution is too coarse: the
label predicts the \emph{average} patient in a class, not the individual in front of the clinician.

Two research programs have grown up in response, and they bet on opposite shapes for psychiatric variation.
The \emph{dimensional} program --- the NIMH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) and the Hierarchical Taxonomy of
Psychopathology (HiTOP)~\cite{insel2010rdoc,kotov2017hitop} --- replaces categories with \emph{continuous
axes} of dysfunction that cut across diagnoses and units of analysis (behaviour, cognition, physiology). The
\emph{biotype} program instead searches for \emph{new discrete subgroups}: data-driven clusters meant to
carve patients more sharply than DSM does~\cite{clementz2016biotypes}.
The two make incompatible claims --- smooth gradients versus latent kinds --- and FACE-ATLAS treats that
disagreement as an empirical question rather than a premise; we return to how the FACE-ATLAS results sit within
both programs, and the wider biomedical literature, in Chapter~\ref{sec:evidence}.

\subsection{Measurement before stratification}
Asking the question cleanly requires separating two problems that are routinely conflated.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Measurement} --- where does each patient sit on a small set of continuous,
  diagnosis-agnostic axes of clinical and biological variation? This \emph{builds the map}.
  \item \textbf{Stratification} --- do those coordinates fall into decision-relevant regions, and are those
  regions \emph{discrete or graded}? This \emph{asks whether the coordinates fall into discrete regions or form a continuum, and how to read a patient's position either way}.
\end{itemize}
Collapsing the two is the classic error. Clustering raw instruments and naming the clusters is a
\emph{theory-imposed scoring system}, not a discovery: it bakes the answer into the choice of features and the
choice of distance. FACE-ATLAS instead estimates the measurement model first and lets stratification act on
the estimated coordinates --- carrying their uncertainty --- only afterwards. Whether the map harbours
biotypes or a continuum is then something the data \emph{answer} (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}), not something the
method assumes.

\subsection{What this report establishes: one thesis, three pillars}
On the harmonized three-cohort FACE baseline ($N=9{,}013$), the program reaches a single result, stated here in
advance so that the rest of the report reads as its defence.

\begin{thesisbox}
Reorganizing severe mental illness around \emph{continuous, diagnosis-agnostic latent dimensions} --- rather
than diagnostic categories --- yields a measurement map that is real, stable, and transdiagnostic; its
\textbf{archetype} geometry turns that map into an interpretable, clinically legible object; and
\textbf{operating in that latent space} delivers durable biology, group-level functional prognosis, and a
principled path toward clinical utility that the categorical default cannot reach. The value is demonstrated
wherever the data can carry it, and bounded honestly wherever they cannot.
\end{thesisbox}

The argument is built on \textbf{three pillars}, and the report is organized around them.

\textbf{Pillar~I --- dimensional analysis (Chapters~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:scoring}).} A dimensional reading
recovers structure the categorical label discards. The load-bearing payoff is a \textbf{three-way dissociation,
biology $\perp$ symptoms $\perp$ severity}: metabolic and inflammatory burden are the \emph{least
severity-entangled} domains in the map (correlating with the general functional-burden factor $G$ at only
$0.12$ and $0.07$, against $0.39$ for cognition and $0.42$ for sleep), and they are \emph{equally} weakly tied
to the symptom axes (their couplings to suicidality, sleep, and mania sit at the $\approx 0.10$ off-diagonal
floor) --- both legs surviving adjustment for medication, adiposity, and site. Biological risk therefore rides
on an axis that neither a clinician's global-severity impression nor the patient's symptom profile reveals.
From a precision-psychiatry standpoint this is the crux: you cannot read a patient's biology off how ill they
look or which symptoms they report. The map is also transdiagnostic (adjusted Rand $\approx 0$ vs.\ the DSM-5
subtypes) and a \emph{tighter} description of the patients than diagnosis. This is the foundation; the next two
pillars build on it.

\textbf{Pillar~II --- archetypes (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}).} The map's shape is settled by evidence, not
assumed. Projected onto the nine coordinates, patients do \emph{not} fall into clean clusters: the space is a
graded \emph{continuum}. The decisive check is a \textbf{single-Gaussian falsification null} --- we ask how
clustered a deliberately structureless cloud (one smooth Gaussian blob with our data's shape) would \emph{look}
to the same tools, and the real data separate no better (best silhouette $0.140$ vs.\ $0.141\pm0.002$). What
organizes the continuum is a small set of \textbf{four extreme phenotypes} --- an $A{=}4$ archetype simplex in
which biology, symptoms, and severity vary nearly independently and every patient is a soft blend rather than a
member of a box. The archetypes are what make the map \emph{readable}: they compress a nine-vector into a few
clinically nameable weights and render the biology$\perp$severity dissociation as two patients you can point to.
(When hard groups are unavoidable, a nested $K$-family is exported with \textbf{no privileged $K$} --- the right
number of regions is an outcome question, answered in Pillar~III: none.)

\textbf{Pillar~III --- operating in the latent space (Chapters~\ref{sec:temporal}--\ref{sec:evidence}).} The
payoff of working in these coordinates is what the categorical default cannot reach. Scored forward onto
follow-up without re-estimation, the geometry \emph{persists} as \emph{durable biology, moving symptoms}
(metabolic test--retest $\text{ICC}=0.91$). It \emph{forecasts} two-year \textbf{functioning} beyond diagnosis,
severity, and the baseline outcome (the archetype phenotype adds $\Delta\text{ELPD}=+59$; functional remission
runs $27\%\!\to\!60\%$ across the corners; it is co-informative with DSM-5), and it is a \emph{sufficient,
structurally faithful} compression of the $143$ raw indicators. These are real clinical objects: group-level
stratification, continuous functional forecasting, and a monitoring atlas.

A word on stance, because it is the difference between a persuasive report and an over-sold one. \emph{The value
leads, and the bounds are stated as plainly as the gains} --- not buried, and not inflated. Scientific validity
(a real, stable, transdiagnostic map carrying genuine, robustness-surviving prognostic signal) is firmly within
reach of this baseline cohort. Strong \emph{individual-level} utility is not yet: the two-year
functional-remission gain over diagnosis, severity, and the baseline outcome is only $\mathrm{AUC}\,+0.011$; the
signal is course-dependent; and on observational treatment-as-usual the map does not reliably tell a clinician
what to prescribe (lithium in bipolar disorder, the cleanest contrast, is a well-powered \emph{null}). Each of
these limits is not a shortfall but a precisely drawn edge that names a defined next study --- and a study that
reports its modest and null results as carefully as its positive ones is, in a field prone to advancing
biological-validity evidence as though it were clinical utility, itself part of the contribution.

\subsection{The method in one paragraph: hybrid discovery}
The map is estimated by a \emph{single} global, missingness-aware Bayesian sparse bifactor / ESEM model ---
a factor model with one general axis touching every indicator (a general dimension of the kind reported across
psychopathology~\cite{caspi2014pfactor}), plus specific axes for each domain, which
additionally permits small data-driven cross-loadings (Chapter~\ref{sec:model}) --- with mixed likelihoods,
fit to the baseline visit. The candidate ontology is an \emph{eligibility map plus soft priors}, not a fixed
set of labels. It first says which constructs are actually measurable in the FACE common variables; constructs
with no valid indicators are declared \code{not_testable} before fitting. For the eligible constructs, theory
says where each instrument \emph{should} load, while the likelihood is free to confirm the axis, split it,
downgrade it to a proxy, or reject it. The soft priors are the formal mechanism of ``theory proposes, data
decides'': most cross-loadings are shrunk toward zero, but the likelihood can pull any of them away if the data
demand it.

\begin{definitionbox}[Three objects kept distinct throughout]
\textbf{Candidate construct} --- a clinical idea, screened for indicators and, if eligible, encoded as soft
loading priors.\quad
\textbf{Empirical dimension} --- a stable, data-supported axis of covariance in the measurement map.\quad
\textbf{Patient stratum} --- a downstream representation of the fixed coordinates: archetype mixtures and a
coarse soft tessellation (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}). A candidate is a hypothesis; a dimension is a measurement
result; a stratum is a validated description of the map's geometry; on this map it is a \emph{reading lens} on a continuum, not a discrete kind.
\end{definitionbox}

The discovery pipeline therefore has two gates (Figure~\ref{fig:discovery}). \textbf{Ten constructs were
originally proposed.} Three of them --- impulsivity, negative symptoms, and sensory abnormalities --- carry no
FACE common-variable indicator and are reported \code{not_testable}; stating that is itself a result, not a
failure. The eligible constructs then enter \emph{one} global fit. That fit placed overall severity as the
general factor $G$, confirmed cognition, sleep, and suicidality, \emph{split} the single
metabolism/immunometabolism construct into two weakly-correlated axes (metabolic and inflammatory), and
downgraded neurodevelopment to a \emph{developmental-risk proxy}; anhedonia, carried by a single thin
indicator, proved inseparable from $G$ and the depression windows and was \emph{rejected}. Two further
constructs --- \textbf{mania} and \textbf{substance use} --- were added once their indicators were ingested into
the harmonized dataset, re-entered through the same global model, and both were confirmed. The measurement map
is therefore \textbf{nine dimensions}:
$G$ plus cognition, metabolic, inflammatory, sleep, developmental-risk, suicidality, mania, and substance ---
discovered axes, not strata.

\subsection{Three pillars, five tests}
The three pillars are not three separate studies; they are one argument, advanced by \emph{five dependent
tests}, each of which can be asked only once the previous one is answered \emph{yes}. The order is logical, not
administrative. The conceptual pipeline runs in four layers (cohorts $\to$ dimensions $\to$ strata $\to$
prognosis/treatment); the five tests walk through it, and each is a chapter. The pillars group those tests by
the claim they serve.

\programstrip{0}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Pillar~I --- dimensional analysis.}\quad \emph{Does the map exist?} The transdiagnostic
  measurement map, built on the baseline visit (Chapters~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:scoring}).
  \item \textbf{Pillar~II --- archetypes.}\quad \emph{How are the coordinates organized} --- discrete types, or
  a graded continuum, and how do we read a patient's position? (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}).
  \item \textbf{Pillar~III --- operating in the latent space.}\quad Three tests of the payoff: \emph{do the
  coordinates persist?} (temporal coherence, Chapter~\ref{sec:temporal}); \emph{do they predict?} (prognosis
  beyond diagnosis and severity, Chapter~\ref{sec:prognosis}); \emph{do they guide treatment?} (moderation,
  Chapter~\ref{sec:treatment}).
\end{itemize}

Each pillar opens with its own claim$\to$evidence$\to$significance divider, so the reader always knows which
part of the thesis a chapter is defending. This document is the methods-of-record and the as-built record,
written at a level of detail so that it can both seed the eventual manuscript and serve as the project's
standing technical reference.

\subsection{Load-bearing invariants}
Four commitments are enforced at every layer of the system, from the data loader to the likelihood. They are
the difference between a defensible map and an artefactual one.

\begin{invariantbox}
\textbf{(I1) No naive imputation.} Structure is estimated from each patient's \emph{observed} cells via an
observed-data likelihood; no completed patient$\times$variable matrix is ever created. Naive imputation
manufactures covariance and fabricates strata when missingness is cohort-, site-, or severity-dependent --- as
it is in FACE.\\[3pt]
\textbf{(I2) Diagnosis is metadata.} BP/SZ/DR labels are never indicators. They enter only as a post-hoc validation layer.\\[3pt]
\textbf{(I3) Baseline defines; follow-up validates.} Dimensions are estimated on the baseline visit V0 only;
visits V1--V4 are reserved for temporal coherence and prognosis, used \emph{after} the map is fixed.\\[3pt]
\textbf{(I4) One global structure.} There is \emph{one} global factor structure on one harmonized matrix;
there are no cohort-specific ``modules.'' An instrument absent for a cohort is simply a missing cell under the
observed-data likelihood, and a candidate with no valid indicators is reported \code{not_testable}, never
back-filled with an invented proxy.
\end{invariantbox}
