\section{Confirmation and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

A measurement claim is only as good as its adversarial checking. The nine-dimension map is hardened along five
arms, each asking a different ``could this be an artefact?'' question: of the \emph{prior} or \emph{estimator}
(§\ref{sub:confirm}), of the \emph{sampler} (§\ref{sub:cert}), of \emph{resampling / cohort imbalance}
(§\ref{sub:robust}), of \emph{cross-cohort non-comparability} (§\ref{sub:invariance}), and of \emph{absolute
misfit} (§\ref{sub:ppc}). Because the marginalized model and FIML share one observed-data objective
(Section~\ref{sec:estimation}), a separate FIML arm is redundant; confirmation is done \emph{in-engine}.

\subsection{Not a prior or estimator artefact}
\label{sub:confirm}
\certified\ If the soft priors were doing the work, a \emph{flat-prior} refit (identification constraints only)
would move the loadings. It does not: the prior-free fit reproduces the soft-prior loadings and $\Phi$
\emph{exactly} --- per-factor Tucker congruence $\varphi = 1.00$, $\max|\Delta\Phi_{\text{off-diag}}| = 0.00$.
Since a flat-prior MAP equals the MLE equals FIML, the structure is \textbf{earned from the data, not
manufactured by the priors}. And the bifactor structure itself is justified by out-of-sample fit: WAIC
decisively prefers it over both simpler alternatives (Figure~\ref{fig:waic}).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/fig_waic.png}
\caption{WAIC model comparison on a common cohort-balanced sub-sample (lower = better out-of-sample fit). The
bifactor ($G$ + specifics) is preferred over a correlated-factors model with no general factor (by $\approx
2{,}700$) and overwhelmingly over a unidimensional null (by $\approx 53{,}000$). Both a general factor
\emph{and} the specific axes are needed.}
\label{fig:waic}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Certified at the largest N that mixes}
\label{sub:cert}
\certified\ The reported nine-dimension fit passes the full sampler battery across seeds:
$\max\widehat{R}\le 1.04$, $\min\mathrm{ESS}\ge 112$, $0$ divergences, $\mathrm{BFMI}\ge 0.41$ (no funnel
pathology), and a \textbf{cross-seed Tucker congruence of $0.993$} (max loading shift $0.19$). The continuous
backbone and the biology$\perp$$G$ estimand mix cleanly at full $N$ ($\widehat{R}=1.010$); the
explicit-latent block (the suicidality$\sim$developmental $\Phi$ coupling) is the documented mixing limit, so
the joint map is certified at the largest $N$ that mixes with this resample-stability guard --- its point
estimates are resample-stable, only the $\Phi$-precision of that one coupling is provisional. Full-$N$
full-precision certification of the explicit block is additional sampling effort, not a change of method.

\subsection{Robust to resampling, cohort imbalance, and site}
\label{sub:robust}
\certified\ Could the map be an artefact of the $11\!:\!1$ BP$:$DR imbalance, of one dominant cohort, or of
recruitment-site clustering? Four resampling arms say no. Under \emph{leave-one-cohort-out}, \emph{diagnosis-
balanced subsampling}, \emph{site cluster-bootstrap}, and a \emph{$1/n_{\text{cohort}}$-weighted} fit, the
minimum Tucker congruence against the full-sample reference is $\mathbf{0.958}$. The structure is not driven by
cohort imbalance, by any single cohort, or by site.

\subsection{Measurement invariance --- largely invariant, with honest partials}
\label{sub:invariance}
\certified\ Does each loading mean the same thing in each cohort? Per-cohort fits with Tucker congruence
(Figure~\ref{fig:invariance}, left) show the core is \textbf{largely invariant}: cognition, metabolic, and
sleep recover congruently in every pair ($\varphi\ge 0.95$), with $12/15$ comparisons fully invariant. The
\emph{partials are documented, not hidden} --- they are themselves findings about where an instrument means
different things across populations:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{$G$ in BP--SZ} ($\varphi 0.92$): SZ lacks the FAST behavioural anchor, so $G$ re-anchors on
  CGI-S/EGF/EQ-5D there.
  \item \textbf{inflammatory in DR} ($\varphi 0.71/0.75$): a real immune re-weighting --- neutrophils load
  $\approx 0$ in DR ($0.07$ vs $0.88$ in BP/SZ) while eosinophils dominate.
  \item \textbf{mania-Altman in DR} ($\varphi 0.764$): clinician-rated YMRS holds across cohorts ($0.57/0.41$),
  but the \emph{self-rated} Altman floors out in a depression-at-risk cohort ($0.76\to0.10$) --- lean on YMRS
  for mania in DR (Figure~\ref{fig:invariance}, right).
  \item \textbf{substance} is invariant BP--SZ ($\varphi 0.997$) and is declared a \textbf{2-cohort axis} (its
  alcohol/cannabis SUD are BP/SZ-only; the observed-likelihood simply omits DR, not claimed invariant there).
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_invariance.png}
\caption{Measurement invariance across BP/SZ/DR. \emph{Left:} Tucker congruence $\varphi$ of the primary
loadings per factor per cohort-pair (green $\ge 0.95$ invariant; amber partial; red non-invariant) --- the lone
red block is inflammatory in DR. \emph{Right:} per-cohort loadings of the new mania and substance indicators;

\end{figure}

\subsection{Absolute fit across both likelihood blocks}
\label{sub:ppc}
\certified\ Posterior-predictive checks ask whether the model can \emph{regenerate} the observed data. The
continuous block has a Bayesian $\mathrm{SRMR}\approx 0.074$ (conventional good fit $<0.08$), with residual
misfit confined to repeated-measure clusters (the same quantity measured twice --- chol/LDL, supine/standing
blood pressure and heart rate, AST/ALT, WAIS subtests), which is method-factor structure, not dimensional
misfit. The non-Gaussian block reproduces \textbf{21 of 22 indicators}' observed endorsement rates / means
within the $90\%$ posterior-predictive interval (Bayesian $p\approx 0.5$; Figure~\ref{fig:ppc}).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/fig_ppc.png}
\caption{Absolute fit of the non-Gaussian block: observed endorsement rate (dot) vs the $90\%$
posterior-predictive interval (bar), per binary/ordinal indicator, coloured by home factor. Every shown
indicator reproduces. The lone failure (a count item, off-scale here) is the suicide-attempt count
\code{isf09a}.}
\label{fig:ppc}
\end{figure}

\begin{caveatbox}[One item-level mis-fit, isolated and flagged]
The single exception is \code{isf09a}, the suicide-attempt \emph{count} --- $90.8\%$ zeros, a hurdle a plain
negative binomial over-predicts in the high-suicidality tail. This is an \textbf{item-level} caveat, not a
factor-level one: the suicidality dimension is carried by its seven binary ISF items, all of which reproduce
(Bayesian $p$ $0.48$--$0.59$). A hurdle / zero-inflated likelihood for that one item is the fix if its count
precision is ever required.
\end{caveatbox}
