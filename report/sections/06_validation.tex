\section{Confirmation and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

A measurement claim is only as good as its adversarial checking. It is not enough to fit a nine-dimension map
and report that it converged; we have to ask, repeatedly and in good faith, ``what \emph{else} could have
produced this picture?'' Every plausible alternative explanation that we cannot rule out is a crack in the
foundation, because everything downstream --- the strata of Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}, the temporal-coherence
checks of Chapter~\ref{sec:temporal}, the prognostic models of Chapter~\ref{sec:prognosis}, and the
treatment-moderation analysis of Chapter~\ref{sec:treatment} --- inherits whatever structure the measurement
model invents. A map that is partly an artefact of the priors, the sampler, or the cohort mix would quietly
contaminate all of it.

So we harden the map along six arms, each isolating one ``could this be an artefact?'' question:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item of the \emph{prior} or the \emph{estimator} --- did the soft theory priors paint the picture rather
  than the data? (§\ref{sub:confirm})
  \item of \emph{confounding} --- is the biology$\perp$$G$ separation really a medication, adiposity, or site
  signal in disguise? (§\ref{sub:confound})
  \item of the \emph{sampler} --- are the posterior draws numerically trustworthy, or is convergence an
  illusion? (§\ref{sub:cert})
  \item of \emph{resampling and cohort imbalance} --- would a different draw of patients, or dropping a
  cohort, give a different map? (§\ref{sub:robust})
  \item of \emph{cross-cohort non-comparability} --- does each loading mean the same thing in BP, SZ, and DR,
  or are we averaging apples and oranges? (§\ref{sub:invariance})
  \item of \emph{absolute misfit} --- can the model actually \emph{regenerate} data that look like the real
  thing? (§\ref{sub:ppc})
\end{itemize}

One simplification keeps this chapter honest and short. The map is estimated under the Gaussian-copula
observed-data likelihood of Chapter~\ref{sec:model}: each indicator is carried into the model on its
standard-normal rank scale, so a loading is a slope on the Gaussianized rank score, and the whole
nine-dimension covariance is then read through the same marginalized Woodbury machinery. Because that
marginalized likelihood and full-information maximum likelihood (FIML) optimize the \emph{same} observed-data
objective (Chapter~\ref{sec:estimation}), a separate FIML confirmation arm would re-ask a question we have
already answered. Confirmation is therefore done \emph{in-engine}: a prior-free refit, model comparison, and
posterior-predictive checks, all on the same copula objects that produced the map. Nothing in this chapter
appeals to a second pipeline; these are the results.

\begin{definitionbox}[What ``artefact'' means here, concretely]
An \emph{artefact} is any feature of the reported map that would change if we changed something that should be
scientifically irrelevant: the prior, the random seed, the particular patients sampled, or the cohort
labels. The biology$\perp$$G$ separation, the split of biology into metabolic and inflammatory axes, and the
mania/substance axes are scientific claims only if they \emph{survive} those changes. Each arm below perturbs
one such irrelevant thing and measures how far the map moves.
\end{definitionbox}

\subsection{Not a prior or estimator artefact}
\label{sub:confirm}
\certified\ Theory entered the model softly, through the loading priors of Chapter~\ref{sec:model}: BMI is
\emph{expected} to define metabolism, CRP to define inflammation, FAST to anchor general burden. A fair
sceptic asks whether those priors did the work --- whether we found inflammation because the data carry it, or
merely because we told the model to look there. The clean test is to delete the theory and refit.

Concretely, we run a \emph{flat-prior} refit: the soft priors are replaced by identification constraints only
(sign-oriented home cells so the factors point the right way; signed cells centered at zero with no shrinkage),
a fresh seed, and no warm-start from the primary fit. If the priors were painting the picture, removing them
would smear it. They do not. The prior-free fit reproduces the soft-prior loadings and the factor-correlation
matrix $\Phi$ \emph{exactly} --- per-factor \emph{Tucker congruence} $\varphi = 1.00$ and
$\max|\Delta\Phi_{\text{off-diag}}| = 0.00$.

\begin{definitionbox}[Tucker congruence $\varphi$]
$\varphi$ is the cosine similarity between two columns of loadings --- the same factor estimated two ways.
It runs from $0$ (orthogonal, unrelated) to $1$ (identical pattern). The field's working threshold is
$\varphi\ge 0.95$ for ``the same factor.'' A value of $1.00$ to two decimals means the flat-prior and
soft-prior factors are, for practical purposes, the same object.
\end{definitionbox}

Why is this conclusive rather than merely reassuring? Because a flat-prior MAP estimate \emph{is} the maximum
likelihood estimate, which \emph{is} FIML on the observed cells. So a flat-prior fit that lands on the same
loadings tells us the structure is \textbf{earned from the data, not manufactured by the priors} --- and it
does so without ever running a separate FIML program. The soft priors only break the rotational tie of
Chapter~\ref{sec:model}; they do not invent the inflammation axis.

That settles \emph{whether the priors did the work}. A second, independent question is whether a general
factor plus specific axes is even the right \emph{shape} of model. We answer it by out-of-sample fit. The
\emph{WAIC} (widely-applicable information criterion --- an estimate of how well a model predicts unseen
data, where lower is better) decisively prefers the bifactor over both simpler alternatives
(Figure~\ref{fig:waic}): a single general factor is too coarse, a bag of correlated factors with no $G$ misses
the shared burden axis, and only the bifactor --- a hub $G$ plus orthogonal spokes --- captures both.

\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/fig_waic.png}
\caption{WAIC model comparison on a common cohort-balanced sub-sample (lower = better out-of-sample fit). The
bifactor ($G$ + specifics) is preferred over a correlated-factors model with no general factor (by $\approx

\label{fig:waic}

\begin{keyresult}[The headline separation, quantified rather than asserted]
The load-bearing finding of the map --- that biology is the \emph{least} severity-entangled domain --- is a
statement about the $G$ row of $\Phi$, not a slogan about a picture. In the correlated-$G$ sensitivity model
of Chapter~\ref{sec:model}, where $G$ is freed to correlate with each specific axis, the biological axes stay
nearly orthogonal to functional burden: metabolic$\sim$$G$ $\approx +0.12$ and inflammatory$\sim$$G$ $\approx
+0.07$, against cognition$\sim$$G$ $\approx +0.39$ and sleep$\sim$$G$ $\approx +0.42$. Read plainly: a
patient's metabolic and immune load is almost independent of how impaired they are overall, whereas cognitive
and sleep burden partly track impairment. Because $G$ carries functioning content only and no symptom content,
this is exactly the kind of signal a clinician's global impression cannot see --- and exactly what a
biology-aware stratification (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}) can exploit.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Is biology$\perp$$G$ a confound? medication, adiposity, and site}
\label{sub:confound}
\certified\ The biology$\perp$$G$ result just above is the single most load-bearing claim in the report:
everything downstream treats the biological corner as a \emph{real} axis of illness rather than a re-description
of severity. So it deserves the hardest adversarial question of all --- and it is the one a journal reviewer
asks first: \emph{is the ``biology axis'' actually biology?} Three deflationary alternatives would make the
finding far less interesting, even trivial:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{Medication.} Antipsychotics \emph{cause} metabolic syndrome --- weight gain, dyslipidemia,
  glucose dysregulation. If the metabolic axis mostly tracks who is medicated, then ``metabolic load independent
  of severity'' collapses to ``\emph{drug exposure} independent of current symptoms'' --- a near-tautology, not a
  discovery.
  \item \textbf{Adiposity.} If the metabolic factor is dominated by body weight, ``metabolic$\perp$$G$'' is just
  ``BMI$\perp$$G$.''
  \item \textbf{Site / assay batch.} The three cohorts ran their bloodwork at different sites on different
  platforms. A ``biological axis'' that really loaded on \emph{which laboratory ran the sample} would be a
  between-cohort scale artefact in a transdiagnostic costume.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definitionbox}[Partialling out a confounder, intuitively]
To ``adjust for'' a confounder is to ask a counterfactual: \emph{among patients matched on age, sex, education,
recruitment site, and medication, is metabolic load still independent of severity?} We answer it the direct way
--- by \emph{subtraction}. For each measurement we remove the part a confounder can predict (regress, say, CRP
on age/sex/site/antipsychotic and keep the leftover) and re-estimate the factor correlations on those leftovers,
the \emph{residuals}. The logic is a sieve: if the biology$\perp$$G$ separation were really the confounder
wearing a biological mask, then subtracting the confounder would wash it out and the residuals would show
nothing. If the separation is still there after the confounder's whole footprint has been removed, it was never
the confounder to begin with. Each rung of the ladder below subtracts one more suspect and re-reads
$\Phi(G,\cdot)$ in what remains.
\end{definitionbox}

We run this on the same correlated-$G$ model that produced the headline, partialling each continuous indicator
on a \emph{growing} design of confounders before the factor model is fit.

\begin{methodsbox}[The adjustment, and why it is conservative]
For a Gaussian item, adjusting its mean by the published covariate term $\beta_j^\top c_i$ is
Frisch--Waugh--Lovell-equivalent to \emph{residualizing} the item on the covariate design $c$ before the factor
model: regress the item on $c$, keep the residual, then fit. The marginalized Woodbury kernel is untouched (the
residual is still zero-mean Gaussian). We walk a ladder of designs, each adding the next suspect: \textbf{A0}
nothing (the reported value); \textbf{A1} age(spline)$+$sex$+$education$+$site; \textbf{A2} A1 $+$ antipsychotic
exposure; \textbf{A3} A2 $+$ BMI moved into the covariate block. \textbf{A2 is the headline, and it is
deliberately conservative}: antipsychotics sit on the \emph{causal path} to metabolic load (they are prescribed
for severity \emph{and} they cause metabolic change), so partialling them out removes genuine signal as well as
confounding. Surviving A2 is a strong test, not a lenient one.
\end{methodsbox}

The antipsychotic exposure is the map-independent harmonized drug-class flag of Chapter~\ref{sec:treatment}
(coverage $\approx54\%$; BP lifetime, SZ/DR current; missing values mean-imputed in the \emph{design} only, never
in the data). The model is the same correlated-$G$ marginalized fit, on an $N\approx2{,}000$ diagnosis-balanced
sub-sample, two seeds; arms A0--A2 converge cleanly ($\widehat{R}\le1.02$, $0$ divergences).

\begin{keyresult}[Biology$\perp$$G$ is not a medication, adiposity, or site artefact]
After adjusting every indicator for age, sex, education, \emph{site}, \emph{and antipsychotic exposure}, the
metabolic ($0.069$) and inflammatory ($0.060$) axes remain the \emph{least} severity-entangled domains in the
map --- below cognition ($0.265$) and sleep ($0.361$) by roughly a factor of four, and essentially unchanged from
their unadjusted values. Metabolic$\sim$$G$ does not inflate under adjustment; it \emph{falls}. The deflationary
reading --- ``the biology axis is just who is medicated, or how heavy, or which lab'' --- is not supported: the
severity-independence of biological load is a property of the patients, not of their treatment, their weight, or
their recruitment site.
\end{keyresult}

\begin{caveatbox}[The BMI arm is non-estimable --- and that is itself the finding]
Arm A3 moves BMI out of the metabolic factor and into the covariate block, asking ``is metabolic$\perp$$G$ just
adiposity?'' The fit \emph{degenerates} ($\widehat{R}\,1.83$, $\mathrm{ESS}\approx2$): BMI is one of the metabolic
factor's own primary indicators, so partialling it out partly dissolves the factor it is meant to test --- you
cannot cleanly remove a thing from its own definition. We report this as non-estimable rather than quote a number
from a non-converged chain. The honest content is that metabolic load and adiposity are \emph{not separable} by
this method --- which is exactly why the \textbf{conservative A2} (antipsychotic-adjusted, BMI \emph{kept} as an
indicator) is the headline arm. Two further bounds: antipsychotic adjustment is over-conservative (it can remove
real, drug-induced biology); and a site \emph{dummy} is coarser than the full cross-platform assay/batch
harmonization this baseline data cannot supply. This is an internal robustness result on the measurement
structure, not external validation.
\end{caveatbox}

\subsection{Documented at the largest N that mixes}
\label{sub:cert}
\documented\ A map can be perfectly reproducible and still be numerical nonsense if the sampler never actually
explored the posterior. Convergence diagnostics are the instruments that catch this. They ask three separate
questions: do independent chains, started from different points, agree on the answer ($\widehat{R}$, where
$1.00$ is perfect agreement)? How many genuinely independent samples is the autocorrelated chain worth
(effective sample size, ESS)? And did the Hamiltonian trajectories ever fail to track the posterior geometry
(divergences, and the energy diagnostic BFMI that flags funnel pathologies)?

The reported nine-dimension fit passes the full battery across seeds: $\max\widehat{R}\le 1.04$,
$\min\mathrm{ESS}\ge 112$, $0$ divergences, and $\mathrm{BFMI}\ge 0.41$ (no funnel). More demanding still, it
passes a \emph{cross-seed} reproducibility check: refit from independent random seeds, the loading map returns
with Tucker congruence $0.993$ (largest single-loading shift $0.19$). Independent runs converge on the same
atlas.

There is one honest boundary, and we state it as a boundary rather than smooth it over. The continuous
backbone --- where the biology$\perp$$G$ estimand lives --- mixes cleanly at the full sample
($\widehat{R}=1.010$). The slowest-mixing object is one coupling in the discrete block (the
suicidality$\sim$developmental $\Phi$ entry), whose latent coordinates cannot be marginalized and must be
sampled per patient. We therefore document the joint map at the \textbf{largest $N$ that mixes}, guarded by
the cross-seed stability above. The practical reading: the point estimates are resample-stable; only the
$\Phi$-precision of that single coupling is provisional. Tightening it is more sampling effort, not a change of
method or a change of map.

\begin{methodsbox}[Why ``documented'', not ``certified'', here]
The status pill on this arm is deliberately weaker than on the others. ``Certified'' means a check passed at
full strength; ``documented'' means we are reporting an honest limit rather than claiming we cleared it. The
distinction matters precisely because the rest of the report leans on these coordinates --- a reader is owed
the knowledge that one $\Phi$ entry is sampled to lower precision than the rest, even though it does not touch
the headline biology$\perp$$G$ result.
\end{methodsbox}

\subsection{Robust to resampling, cohort imbalance, and site}
\label{sub:robust}
\certified\ The cohorts are wildly unbalanced --- roughly $11$ bipolar patients for every depression patient
--- and patients are clustered within recruitment sites. A reasonable worry is that the map is really a
portrait of the dominant cohort, or of one large site, dressed up as a transdiagnostic structure. If so, it
would shatter the moment we changed the sampling.

We change the sampling four ways and watch whether the map holds. \emph{Leave-one-cohort-out} asks whether any
single cohort is load-bearing. \emph{Diagnosis-balanced subsampling} forces equal cohort representation,
removing the $11\!:\!1$ tilt. \emph{Site cluster-bootstrap} resamples whole sites, testing for recruitment
artefacts. A \emph{$1/n_{\text{cohort}}$-weighted} fit down-weights the large cohorts directly. Across all
four, the minimum Tucker congruence against the full-sample reference is $\mathbf{0.958}$ --- comfortably above
the $0.95$ ``same factor'' bar. The structure is not driven by cohort imbalance, by any single cohort, or by
site. It is the same map however we slice the patients.

\subsection{Measurement invariance --- largely invariant, with honest partials}
\label{sub:invariance}
\certified\ Invariance is the question of whether a measurement \emph{means the same thing} in different
groups. A loading is a translation rule from a latent axis to an observed item; if that rule differs by cohort,
then comparing a BP patient's metabolic coordinate to an SZ patient's is comparing readings from two
differently-calibrated instruments. We test it by refitting per cohort and comparing the primary loadings with
Tucker congruence (Figure~\ref{fig:invariance}, left).

The core is \textbf{largely invariant}: cognition, metabolic, and sleep recover congruently in every cohort
pair ($\varphi\ge 0.95$), with $12/15$ comparisons fully invariant. The honest exceptions are
\emph{documented, not hidden} --- and each one is itself a finding about where a single instrument means
different things across populations:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{$G$ in BP--SZ} ($\varphi 0.92$): SZ lacks the FAST behavioural anchor, so $G$ re-anchors on
  CGI-S/EGF/EQ-5D there. The general factor still measures functional burden in SZ; it simply leans on
  different anchors.
  \item \textbf{inflammatory in DR} ($\varphi 0.71/0.75$): a genuine immune re-weighting, not a fit failure
  --- neutrophils load $\approx 0$ in DR ($0.07$ vs $0.88$ in BP/SZ) while eosinophils dominate. The immune
  axis exists in DR but is carried by different cells.
  \item \textbf{mania-Altman in DR} ($\varphi 0.764$): clinician-rated YMRS holds across cohorts
  ($0.57/0.41$), but the \emph{self-rated} Altman item floors out in DR ($0.76\to0.10$). Self-reported manic
  activation is near-floor in a depression cohort; lean on YMRS for mania in DR
  (Figure~\ref{fig:invariance}, right).
  \item \textbf{substance} is invariant BP--SZ ($\varphi 0.997$) and is declared a \textbf{2-cohort axis}: its
  alcohol/cannabis lifetime-SUD indicators are BP/SZ-only, so the observed-cell likelihood simply omits DR
  rather than claiming an invariance that the data cannot support.
\end{itemize}

\begin{caveatbox}[How to carry the partials downstream]
The four partials are constraints on interpretation, not on the map's existence. When a later chapter compares
strata across cohorts (Chapter~\ref{sec:strata}) or tracks an axis over time (Chapter~\ref{sec:temporal}), the
inflammatory and mania-Altman comparisons in DR, and the $G$ comparison in SZ, carry their re-anchoring caveat;
substance is read as a BP/SZ-only axis throughout. These are exactly the kinds of cross-cohort subtlety that an
honest transdiagnostic map should surface rather than paper over.
\end{caveatbox}

\subsection{Absolute fit across both likelihood blocks}
\label{sub:ppc}
\certified\ The four arms above are \emph{relative}: they show the map is reproducible, prior-free,
resample-stable, and largely invariant. None of them asks the most basic question of all --- can the model
generate data that actually \emph{look like} the real patients? That is the job of posterior-predictive checks
(PPCs). We draw synthetic datasets from the fitted posterior and compare summary statistics of the synthetic
data to the observed data. A model that cannot reproduce the data it was fit to has misspecified something,
no matter how clean its diagnostics.

We run the check on both halves of the mixed likelihood. For the Gaussianized continuous block, the natural
summary is the model-implied versus observed pairwise correlation structure, condensed into a \emph{Bayesian
SRMR} --- a root-mean-square residual correlation, where the conventional good-fit threshold is $<0.08$. The
map scores $\mathrm{SRMR}\approx 0.074$. The small residual misfit is confined to \emph{repeated-measure}
clusters --- the same quantity measured twice (cholesterol/LDL, supine/standing blood pressure and heart rate,
AST/ALT, WAIS subtests) --- which is method-factor structure (two measurements of one thing share extra
variance), not a failure of the dimensional map. For the non-Gaussian block --- the binary and ordinal items
that keep their native likelihoods --- the check is whether the model reproduces each indicator's observed
endorsement rate or mean. It reproduces \textbf{21 of 22 indicators} within the $90\%$ posterior-predictive
interval (Bayesian $p\approx 0.5$, i.e.\ the observed value sits squarely in the middle of the predicted
range; Figure~\ref{fig:ppc}).

\begin{caveatbox}[One item-level mis-fit, isolated and flagged]
The single exception is \code{isf09a}, the suicide-attempt \emph{count} --- $90.8\%$ zeros, a hurdle a plain
negative binomial over-predicts in the high-suicidality tail. The fix is the right likelihood for that one
item: a hurdle / zero-inflated count, should its count precision ever be needed. Crucially this is an
\textbf{item-level} caveat, not a \textbf{factor-level} one. The suicidality \emph{dimension} is carried by
its seven binary ISF ideation/attempt items, all of which reproduce cleanly (Bayesian $p$ $0.48$--$0.59$).
The axis is sound; one count item on it is mis-specified.
\end{caveatbox}

\begin{keyresult}[What the six arms buy]
Taken together, the arms say the nine-dimension map is \textbf{not} an artefact of the priors (flat-prior refit
$\varphi=1.00$), of \emph{confounding} (biology$\perp$$G$ holds under medication and site adjustment --- metabolic
$0.069$, inflammatory $0.060$), the sampler (cross-seed $\varphi=0.993$, clean diagnostics), the resampling
(worst-case $\varphi=0.958$ across four schemes), or cross-cohort non-comparability (largely invariant, with four
documented and interpretable partials), and it \textbf{can} regenerate the observed data on both likelihood blocks
(SRMR $0.074$; 21/22 discrete indicators reproduce). The biology$\perp$$G$ separation, the metabolic/inflammatory
split, and the mania and substance axes are therefore properties of the data, not of our modelling choices ---
a substrate solid enough to build the strata, temporal, prognosis, and treatment milestones on
(Chapters~\ref{sec:strata}--\ref{sec:treatment}).
\end{keyresult}
