\section{Validated strata: a transdiagnostic continuum}
\label{sec:strata}

The previous chapters gave us a map: every one of the $9{,}013$ patients placed on nine dimensions ---
severity, two kinds of biology (metabolic, inflammatory), cognition, sleep, mania, suicidality, developmental
adversity, substance --- with diagnosis deliberately held out. This chapter asks the question the whole
``precision psychiatry'' field chases: do patients fall into a handful of natural \emph{types} --- biotypes ---
that cut across the old diagnostic labels? Half the literature claims exactly this (``we found three biotypes
of depression,'' and so on). The discipline here is to \emph{not assume the answer}.

Everything below is computed on the patient coordinates the measurement model produced --- the same posterior
draws, with each patient's per-patient uncertainty carried forward, never collapsed to a point and never
imputed. Where the map chapter learned \emph{where} each patient sits, this chapter asks what \emph{shape} the
cloud of patients makes.

\subsection{Ask whether structure exists before imposing it}
The classic error in clustering clinical data is to pick a number of clusters $K$ and then ``find'' $K$
groups --- the algorithm always returns them, whether or not the data contain any. So before doing any
clustering, we put the strongest possible question to the data (Figure~\ref{fig:gateflow}): \emph{can the best
possible partition of these patients separate them any better than the best possible partition of a cloud that
has no structure at all?} If it cannot, there are no biotypes to find, and a biotype claim is inadmissible.

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\structuregateflow
\caption{Falsification-first. Before clustering, the best partition of the real coordinates is compared against
the best partition of a single Gaussian blob with the same mean and covariance --- a cloud that has \emph{no}
clusters by construction. The real data separate no better ($z=0.1$): the verdict is a \emph{continuum}, which
is then represented two complementary, uncertainty-propagating ways --- four extreme phenotypes (the
load-bearing view) and a coarse soft tessellation exported as a nested $K$-family with no privileged $K$. The
gate is the stratification analogue of the map chapter's ``the eligibility map is itself a result.''}
\label{fig:gateflow}
\end{figure}

Crucially, every step propagates the map's per-patient \emph{uncertainty} rather than discarding it. The
falsification test runs over the map's posterior draws; the soft tessellation is fit by \textbf{Extreme
Deconvolution}, a measurement-error Gaussian mixture
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xd}
x_i \;\sim\; \sum_{k}\pi_k\,\Normal\!\left(m_k,\;V_k + S_i\right),
\end{equation}
in which each patient's \emph{known} posterior covariance $S_i$ is added inside the component --- so the
mixture clusters what patients \emph{are}, after the noise in how they were measured is accounted for. Because
the copula fit was run at full $N$, the per-patient latents are already in the posterior, so all $9{,}013$
patients are scored on all nine axes directly from the draws --- no re-fit, no projection, no imputation. The
tight axes (metabolic, inflammatory, developmental) carry posterior SD $\approx 0.25$; the thin axes (mania,
substance, suicidality) carry SD $0.48$--$0.68$, and that honest width is propagated everywhere below.

\begin{methodsbox}[Converting a hidden assumption into a tested result]
Asking ``is there structure?'' before clustering turns the buried premise ($K$ biotypes) into a reported
finding. The decisive instrument is a \emph{falsification null}: build a structureless reference (one Gaussian
with the data's own mean and covariance), cluster it as hard as you cluster the real data, and compare the
separation achieved. A continuum is the null hypothesis; only evidence that the real data \emph{beat} the
structureless reference can overturn it. A convergent battery (Hopkins, Mapper, the dip test, HDBSCAN, the
mixture's model evidence) is read in the same conservative spirit.
\end{methodsbox}

\begin{keyresult}[No evidence for well-separated discrete biotypes --- the space is a graded continuum]
The best partition of the real nine-dimensional cloud separates patients \emph{no better} than the best
partition of a single structureless Gaussian blob (silhouette $0.140$ vs.\ $0.140\pm0.003$, $z=0.1$). Five
further methods agree: the main axis is unimodal (dip $p\approx 0.99$), density clustering finds nothing
(HDBSCAN: $0$ clusters), the topology is one connected component (Mapper), and once the per-patient
uncertainty is propagated the optimal number of components collapses to $1$ in all $20$ posterior draws. The
nine-dimensional cloud is one smooth, graded mass; patients spread out continuously rather than clumping into
islands.
\end{keyresult}

This is the central finding of this chapter, and it has teeth precisely because it is honest and contrarian. It
says most published ``biotype'' results are probably artefacts of forcing a cluster count onto data that has
none --- which is exactly why those results famously fail to replicate --- and it lands squarely where serious
psychiatry has been heading (the dimensional RDoC/HiTOP view). It is also a \emph{calibrated} negative, not a
slogan: the GMM-BIC gain over a single Gaussian is large ($10{,}326$), so the cloud is genuinely
\emph{non-Gaussian} --- but the silhouette proves those extra components are not \emph{separated}, so the
non-Gaussianity is \emph{shape} (skew and the archetype corners we meet next), not clusters. What we cannot
rule out is structure hiding in the raw indicators that the nine-dimensional summary blurs, or structure that
emerges only with follow-up and outcomes; that is precisely what the temporal and prognosis chapters test. The
continuum claim is about \emph{this} baseline measurement map.

\subsection{A continuum still has a shape: four extreme phenotypes}
\label{sub:archetypes}
No clusters does not mean no structure. A continuum still has a \emph{shape}, and the shape has corners ---
\textbf{extreme phenotypes}.

\begin{definitionbox}[The colour-palette analogy]
There is no discrete set of ``the colours,'' yet there are pure extremes --- the primaries --- and every real
colour is a blend of them. Likewise the patient cloud has no natural groups, but it has \emph{corners}: pure
extreme phenotypes, with every patient a blend. The right description of a patient is not ``which box?'' but
``what mixture of extremes?'' --- both more honest and closer to how clinicians actually think.
\end{definitionbox}

How many corners? On a continuum the explained variance always rewards adding more, so parsimony alone cannot
choose. We instead pick the largest number of archetypes that \emph{reproduces} across random seeds --- the
largest $A$ whose cross-seed Tucker congruence stays above $0.8$. Stability is non-monotonic, with islands at
$A=2$ (congruence $0.999$) and $A=4$ (congruence $0.979$); $A=3,5,6,7,8$ all collapse below $0.5$, chasing the
wide posterior uncertainty on the thin axes rather than recovering real corners. The operational choice is
therefore $A=4$ (explained variance $0.52$): the largest stable resolution.

\begin{methodsbox}[Why ``the stable resolution,'' not ``the most variance''?]
Archetypal analysis fits the convex hull of the cloud, so each added corner explains a little more --- there is
never an elbow that announces ``this many.'' The honest selector is reproducibility: a corner that is real
re-appears when you reshuffle and refit; a corner that is fitting noise jumps around. On the copula
coordinates, only $A=2$ and $A=4$ survive that test. The wider, honest uncertainty on suicidality, substance,
and developmental is exactly what destabilizes higher resolutions --- the model declines to invent corners it
cannot place reliably.
\end{methodsbox}

The four stable extreme phenotypes (Table~\ref{tab:archetypes}, Figure~\ref{fig:archetypes}) are not the nine
axis-extremes one might naively expect. They are four \emph{combinations} that separate biology from symptoms
from severity.

Each corner deserves a sentence, because together they are the whole transdiagnostic story.

\textbf{A0 --- the biological corner.} The cardiometabolic--inflammatory extreme: high metabolic ($+1.99$),
inflammatory ($+2.52$) and substance ($+2.55$) load, with elevated severity ($+1.61$). This is the corner the
entire project exists to find --- patients whose biology runs hot --- and it survives all the way into the
shape of the continuum. Roughly a quarter of patients have A0 as their dominant blend.

\textbf{A1 --- the low-burden pole.} Everything low; the ``healthy reference'' extreme. Severity sits at
$-1.94$, sleep and developmental adversity near $-1.65$. This is the modal patient (dominant for $36\%$): the
calm end of the continuum from which the other corners are departures.

\textbf{A2 --- the severe, non-biological corner.} High overall severity ($+1.80$) but the \emph{lowest}
inflammatory ($-2.93$) and substance ($-2.99$) load on the map. A2 is the proof, written into the geometry,
that a patient can be very ill with their biology dissociated --- severity is not biology.

\textbf{A3 --- the psychiatric-symptom corner.} High sleep ($+2.24$), developmental adversity ($+2.18$), mania
($+1.30$) and suicidality ($+1.03$), with \emph{low} metabolic ($-1.75$). The symptom extreme: psychiatric
burden running high while the biology runs low --- the mirror image of A0 on the biology axes.

\begin{keyresult}[Biology $\perp$ symptoms $\perp$ severity --- three quasi-independent axes of extremity]
The biological extreme (A0) and the psychiatric-symptom extreme (A3) are \emph{separate} corners, and A2 shows
severity rising while biology falls. So the continuum has (at least) three quasi-independent directions of
extremity: \emph{how much} (severity), \emph{which biology} (metabolic/inflammatory/substance), and
\emph{which symptoms} (sleep/developmental/mania/suicidality). Two patients who look equally ill can sit at
biologically \emph{opposite} corners. A map that only knew ``how sick are they?'' would be blind to this --- and
that heterogeneity is exactly what a biology-aware stratification is supposed to capture.
\end{keyresult}

The other half of the continuum story is the blending. Most patients do not \emph{sit} at a corner
(Figure~\ref{fig:simplex}).

\begin{keyresult}[Most patients are blends, not corners]
$61\%$ of patients have no single dominant archetype (max simplex weight $<0.5$; mean archetype entropy
$0.78$): they live in the \emph{interior} between corners, and even ``dominant'' patients average only $\approx
0.48$ weight on their lead corner. Hard assignment would be a fiction --- the honest decision object is the
\emph{distribution} over phenotypes, exactly what the continuum and the map's per-patient uncertainty demand.
This is why the archetype simplex (and the continuous coordinates), not any hard tessellation, is the
load-bearing object the later chapters consume.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Severity is the spine, not a corner}
\label{sub:spine}
The most elegant detail: \textbf{overall severity has no corner of its own.} Geometrically it is the
\emph{spine} --- the axis the entire cloud is stretched along (Figure~\ref{fig:simplex}) --- and every
archetype sits at some severity level. This is the bifactor structure of the map made visible as geometry:
severity is the \emph{how much} ($G$, functional burden, with no symptom content of its own), the specific
axes (biology, cognition, sleep, mania, \dots) are the \emph{what kind}, and they are largely independent. So
the ``biological'' corner (A0, severity $+1.61$) and the ``severe non-biological'' corner (A2, severity
$+1.80$) sit at \emph{almost the same} severity --- they differ in what is wrong, not how much. This is
geometry recovering the very orthogonality the measurement model built in: biology is the least
severity-entangled signal on the map (metabolic$\,\sim\,G$ correlation $+0.12$, inflammatory $+0.07$, against
cognition $0.39$ and sleep $0.42$).

\subsection{A coarse hard label, if you want one: a nested $K$-family with no privileged $K$}
\label{sub:tessellation}
The continuous coordinates and the archetype simplex are the scientific objects. But operations sometimes want
a coarse hard label --- a small set of decision regions. On a continuum there is no \emph{correct} number of
regions, so we do not crown one: we fit a soft (measurement-error) tessellation and export a \textbf{nested
$K$-family} ($K=2,3,4$), deferring the choice of an operative $K$ --- if any earns its keep --- to the
incremental-validity test in the prognosis chapter (Chapter~\ref{sec:prognosis}).

\begin{definitionbox}[Why a $K$-family, not a $K$]
The mixture's own evidence (the cross-validated XD-BIC) is essentially \emph{flat} across $K$:
$197{,}918$--$199{,}505$ over $K=2$--$8$, a spread under $1\%$, with the nominal minimum at $K=3$. A flat basin
\emph{is} the continuum signature --- there is no natural number of regions, and an internal tiebreak would be
false precision. So we ship the family and let external (predictive) validity decide. $K=2$ is exported as the
\emph{contract default} the downstream pipeline needs, not a privileged scientific choice.
\end{definitionbox}

What does the coarse split cut on? At every $K$ it cuts \emph{first} on psychiatric symptom burden, not on
biology and not on severity (Table~\ref{tab:kfamily}, Figure~\ref{fig:regions}). At the $K=2$ contract default
the two regions are near mirror-images on the symptom axes and essentially \emph{zero} on biology and severity:
\textbf{R0} ``near-average'' ($57\%$ of patients) sits at suicidality $-0.54$, mania $-0.24$, substance
$-0.26$, sleep $-0.22$; \textbf{R1} ``$\uparrow$suicidality'' ($43\%$) is the reflection (suicidality $+0.62$,
mania $+0.28$, substance $+0.26$, sleep $+0.25$). The split's variance-explained is led overwhelmingly by
suicidality ($\eta^2\,0.543$) and the symptom cluster, while severity and biology are untouched.

\begin{keyresult}[The coarse split is symptom burden, not severity and not biology]
The $K=2$ partition explains $\eta^2\,0.122$ of the \emph{specific} (symptom) axes but only $\eta^2\,0.008$ of
$G$ (severity) --- so it is \emph{not} a severity ladder, which is why the ``more than severity'' gate passes.
It is equally blind to biology (metabolic $\eta^2\,0.003$, inflammatory $0.004$). The coarsest operational
label of this continuum is a low- versus elevated-symptom-burden split, anchored by suicidality, that says
nothing about how ill or how biologically hot a patient is.
\end{keyresult}

This is the load-bearing reconciliation, and it is not a contradiction --- it is the continuum signature. A
mixture partitions by \emph{density}: it cuts where the cloud is most separable into regions, which is the
high-variance, suicidality-anchored symptom direction. Biology has \emph{no} density gap --- patients vary
continuously along metabolic and inflammatory --- so biology never produces a region boundary at any $K$. The
archetypes, by contrast, find \emph{extremes}, not splits, and biology is a genuine direction of maximal
phenotype (the A0 corner). \emph{There are no biology clusters (so biology drives no $K$-split), but there are
biology extremes (so biology defines an archetype corner).} That is exactly why the archetypes and the
continuous coordinates --- not the tessellation --- are the object that carries biology forward.

\subsection{The DSM-5 showdown}
We then put the map head-to-head against the diagnostic labels --- the cohorts and the DSM-5 subtypes
(Table~\ref{tab:m2valid}, Figure~\ref{fig:embedding}). Two clean results.

\textbf{The structure shares almost zero information with DSM-5.} The adjusted Rand index --- a measure of how
much two groupings agree, scaled so that $0$ is chance agreement and $1$ is identical --- between the strata
and diagnosis is $\approx 0$ ($0.00$ vs.\ cohort, $0.01$ vs.\ DSM-5). Every region and every archetype is a
full mix of diagnoses: the symptom corner (A3) is the most bipolar-leaning at $82\%$ BP and the severe corner
(A2) the most schizophrenia-enriched at $37\%$ SZ, yet \emph{none} is a single diagnosis. The diagnoses are
shuffled right through the cloud.

\textbf{The map describes patients far better than the labels.} A free soft tessellation beats a
DSM-5-constrained mixture on model evidence --- XD-BIC $197{,}963$ versus $201{,}292$, a tighter description
with \emph{fewer} groups --- and explains more than four times the coordinate variance (mean $\eta^2\,0.108$
vs.\ $0.025$). The diagnoses barely organize this biological/cognitive landscape at all.

\begin{caveatbox}[The line we did not cross: ``describes better'' is not ``clinically better'']
We have \emph{not} shown the map predicts outcomes or guides treatment better than diagnosis. Those are the
questions of the prognosis and treatment chapters, and overclaiming them now would be the field's besetting
sin. What this chapter earns is a \emph{descriptive} win and the right setup for the real test --- and it
reframes the question sharply: not ``are our clusters the true kinds?'' but ``does a continuous, biology-aware
representation out-predict the DSM-5 subtypes on course and treatment response?''
\end{caveatbox}

\subsection{Why trust it: the structure is about patients, not measurement}
\begin{keyresult}[Membership is not a missingness artefact]
The classic trap in clustering messy clinical data is accidentally grouping patients by \emph{who got which
tests} rather than what they are like --- people with bloodwork versus without form fake ``biotypes.'' We
tested exactly that: a classifier predicting a patient's region from the \emph{pattern of what was measured}
yields a \emph{negative} lift ($-0.055$, permutation $p=0.60$), worse than chance. Membership is governed by
what patients \emph{are}, not by what was measured on them. The whole measurement-error apparatus ---
propagating each patient's uncertainty the entire way (Eq.~\ref{eq:xd}) --- did its job, and this vindicates the
map's ``propagate the uncertainty'' design.
\end{keyresult}

Softness, too, is reported honestly rather than hidden by a hard label (Figure~\ref{fig:confidence}). At the
$K=2$ default the median assignment entropy is $0.51$: patients near the suicidality midline are genuine
boundary cases with responsibilities $\approx(0.5,0.5)$, and the hand-off carries each patient's entropy and a
confidence tier so a downstream user can see who is core and who is on a soft edge.

\subsection{The one picture to hold in your head}
The embedding says it all (Figure~\ref{fig:embedding}): one diffuse cloud, with bipolar, schizophrenia, and
depression patients thoroughly intermixed (no diagnostic territories), and smooth gradients crossing it in
different directions --- a severity gradient and a biological gradient. Not a constellation of islands; a
\emph{landscape with a ridge and crossing slopes}.

\subsection{What the stratification establishes --- and what it defers}
This chapter converts the map's coordinate system into decision objects --- soft archetype memberships and a
nested $K$-family of regions, all with uncertainty --- and establishes, under \emph{internal/descriptive}
validity, that they are real, stable, transdiagnostic, specific-axis-driven, not a missingness artefact, and a
tighter description of the data than the diagnostic labels. The honest boundaries are explicit:

\begin{openbox}[Internal/descriptive validity only --- and the operative $K$ is deferred on purpose]
The stratification does \emph{not} claim the strata are natural kinds (they are a continuum), nor any
predictive, prognostic, temporal, or treatment value. The load-bearing objects are the \emph{continuous
coordinates} and the \emph{$A=4$ archetype simplex}; the hard tessellation is a coarse convention shipped as a
nested $K$-family with \textbf{no privileged $K$}. Archetype granularity is honest about its limits --- only
$A=2$ and $A=4$ reproduce across seeds --- and substance is thin (two SUD binaries, no DR data) and carried
with wide uncertainty. Crucially, the choice of an operative $K$ is \emph{deliberately} left open: an internal
parsimony tiebreak on a flat XD-BIC basin would be false precision, so the non-circular way to honour ``choose
$K$ for actionability'' is to let \emph{outcomes} pick it. The decisive tests come in the later chapters:
\textbf{temporal coherence} (Chapter~\ref{sec:temporal} --- do the coordinates and memberships \emph{persist}
across follow-up, and more stably than a DSM-5 diagnosis?), \textbf{prognosis} (Chapter~\ref{sec:prognosis} ---
do the strata add \emph{incremental predictive value beyond diagnosis $+$ severity}, and which $K$, if any,
earns its keep?), and \textbf{treatment} (Chapter~\ref{sec:treatment} --- do the strata \emph{moderate
treatment response}?). This chapter is the description; the later chapters test whether the description is
clinically useful.
\end{openbox}
