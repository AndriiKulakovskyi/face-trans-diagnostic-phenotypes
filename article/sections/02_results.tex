\section{Results}

\subsection{Sample and analytic strategy}

The analysis sample was the harmonized baseline (visit V0) of the three FACE
cohorts: $N=9{,}013$ patients (bipolar disorder [BP] $6{,}252$; schizophrenia [SZ]
$2{,}209$; major depression [DR] $552$) recruited across 21 expert-centre sites
(Table~\ref{tab:characteristics}). The cohorts differ in age, sex distribution and
chronicity, as expected for these disorders. We deliberately fit the measurement
model on the \emph{full} sample without completeness selection; $143$ indicators
were modelled, with a mean cell missingness of $39.8\%$ preserved as missing and
never imputed. Diagnosis (cohort and DSM-5 subtype) was held out of the model and
used only as a validation/metadata label. Follow-up visits at 12 and 24 months
(retained: $47.4\%$ and $32.8\%$ of V0) were used only to test temporal coherence
and prognosis, never to define the map. The full statistical specification is in
Methods; Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the design.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig1_overview.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Study design and the five questions.} The harmonized
  three-cohort FACE baseline (bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, depression;
  $N=9{,}013$) is reorganized around diagnosis-agnostic axes by one global,
  missingness-aware Bayesian bifactor/ESEM model fit to each patient's observed
  cells only, with three load-bearing invariants (no imputation, diagnosis as

  interrogated through five dependent questions---exists, organizes, persists,

\subsection{A certified nine-dimension transdiagnostic map (the map exists)}

From ten candidate constructs, one global model returned a stable nine-dimension
structure: a general functional-burden factor (\Gfac) plus eight weakly correlated
specific axes---cognition, metabolic, inflammatory, sleep, developmental-risk,
suicidality, mania and substance (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas};
Table~\ref{tab:dimensions}). Inter-factor correlations were weak (mean absolute
off-diagonal $\approx 0.10$), confirming genuinely distinct axes rather than one
collapsed dimension. \Gfac{} was anchored only by functioning and global-severity
indicators (FAST loading $0.90$, EGF $0.73$) and carried no symptom content,
supporting its conservative reading as transdiagnostic functional burden rather
than a symptom-liability ``\textit{p}''. Depression and anxiety instruments
(MADRS, QIDS, STAI) did not form a separate factor; they loaded $0.66$--$0.80$ on
\Gfac{} as cross-loading ``windows''. The single prior ``biology'' candidate
\emph{split} into distinct metabolic and inflammatory factors (inter-factor
$\Phi \approx 0.19$). Of the ten candidates, nine were confirmed (one as a split),
anhedonia was rejected, and impulsivity, negative symptoms and sensory abnormality
were \emph{not testable} for lack of common indicators---each verdict an outcome of
the analysis rather than an assumption (Table~\ref{tab:dimensions}). The structure
was not an artefact of the priors: a flat-prior refit reproduced the loadings and
$\Phi$ essentially exactly (Tucker congruence $\varphi = 1.00$), and model
comparison decisively preferred the bifactor over unidimensional and
correlated-factor alternatives. Absolute fit was acceptable across both likelihood
blocks (continuous Bayesian SRMR $\approx 0.07$; $21/22$ non-Gaussian indicators
reproduced within the $90\%$ posterior-predictive interval).

\subsection{Biology is the least severity-entangled domain (the headline)}

Because the bifactor identification fixes \Gfac{}~\orth{}~specifics by construction,
we re-estimated the model allowing \Gfac{} to correlate freely with the specific
axes. Under this correlated-\Gfac{} model, metabolic and inflammatory load were the
\emph{least} entangled with functional burden: correlations with \Gfac{} of
$0.14$ (metabolic) and $0.06$ (inflammatory), against $0.39$ for cognition and
$0.44$ for sleep (Figure~\ref{fig:biologyg}). In other words, two patients with the
same overall impairment can carry very different metabolic and immune loads---a
patient's biological burden rides largely on axes that the global clinical-severity
picture does not capture. Consistent with a large independent literature
(see Discussion), we state this as relative, not absolute, independence: biology is
\emph{largely independent of a general functional-burden axis}, with the
$0.06$--$0.14$ values quantifying---at the low end of what that literature would
predict---a pattern previously described only qualitatively.

\subsection{The patient space is a continuum, not biotypes (the map organizes)}

Before clustering, a pre-registered structure-discovery gate---run over the model's
posterior draws and combining cluster tendency, modality, density, model selection
and topology---returned a unanimous verdict of \emph{no discrete clusters}:
gap-statistic $K=1$; HDBSCAN assigned $100\%$ of points to noise; the first
principal component was unimodal (Hartigan dip $p\approx 0.99$); the archetype
scree showed no elbow; and the mixture BIC formed a flat basin. The
nine-dimensional space is therefore a graded continuum. We represented it two
complementary ways: eight extreme phenotypes (archetypes; cross-seed congruence
$0.999$), each the high pole of one axis plus a low-burden pole, with every patient
a soft convex blend (Figure~\ref{fig:continuum}); and a coarser four-region soft
tessellation. Three properties make the continuum decision-relevant. First, it is
\emph{transdiagnostic}: the adjusted Rand index between the data-driven structure
and diagnosis was $\approx 0$ (tessellation $0.02$ vs the seven DSM-5 subtypes;
archetypes $0.05$), every phenotype mixing all cohorts. Second, it is
\emph{driven by the specific/biological axes, not severity}: partition variance
explained peaked on specific axes (maximum $\eta^2 = 0.45$) above \Gfac{}
($0.31$), and---crucially---distinct metabolic \emph{and} inflammatory corners
emerged, so the biology\,\orth\,\Gfac{} signal survives into the phenotypes, while
overall severity formed no corner of its own (it is the continuum's spine). Third,
it is \emph{not a missingness artefact}: a classifier predicting membership from
the per-axis coverage pattern performed below baseline (lift $-0.08$), and the
partition was seed-stable. The data-driven regions also described the coordinate
cloud more tightly than diagnosis (mixture BIC $199{,}325$ vs $206{,}016$ for a
DSM-5-constrained model; coordinate variance explained $\approx 21\%$ vs
$\approx 5\%$)---a descriptive, not yet clinical, superiority.

\subsection{The geometry persists over two years (the map persists)}

Scored forward onto the 12- and 24-month visits on the \emph{fixed} model
(observed cells, uncertainty propagated, never re-estimated), the measurement
remained invariant for five of six testable backbone axes (Tucker
$\varphi = 0.96$--$1.00$; inflammatory partial at $0.90$), licensing the reading of
coordinate change as patient change rather than instrument drift. A
measurement-error decomposition then separated trait from state
(Figure~\ref{fig:traitstate}). The durable, trait-like axes were the biological
and cognitive ones (rank-stability intraclass correlation: metabolic $0.93$,
inflammatory $0.85$, cognition $0.78$); the moving, state-like axes were the
symptom ones (sleep $0.49$, suicidality $0.46$). At the population level the cohort
slid \emph{down} the severity and symptom axes while remaining static on biology.
A per-patient geometric analysis agreed: severity moved (cleared measurement error)
in $34.5\%$ of patients versus only $20.2\%$ for the biology corner---a
$2.2\times$ edge for ``spine slides, corner holds''---and archetype identity
persisted in $52\%$ of patients ($\kappa = 0.27$ vs a $12.5\%$ chance rate). The
clinical reading is compact: \emph{stratify on the durable biology, monitor the
moving symptoms}.

\subsection{It predicts future functioning, modestly and at the group level (the map predicts)}

We then asked whether a baseline coordinate or phenotype predicts a two-year
outcome \emph{incrementally beyond} diagnosis, current severity and the baseline
value of the outcome, using an errors-in-variables Bayesian model that propagated
each coordinate's measurement uncertainty. The map added predictive signal for
\emph{functioning} but not for severity (which was autoregression-saturated). The
eight archetypes added held-out predictive value for functioning (expected
log-predictive-density gain $+46$), and the durable biological axes were
individually informative: higher metabolic ($\beta = -0.062$, $95\%$ credible
interval $[-0.103,-0.022]$) and inflammatory ($\beta = -0.060$ $[-0.112,-0.011]$)
load predicted worse future functioning, with the metabolic effect surviving
adjustment for the error-corrected severity coordinate---it is not baseline severity
measured noisily. The signal was \emph{co-informative} with DSM-5 (each added beyond
the other) and \emph{course-dependent}, concentrated in the episodic courses
(bipolar disorder, depression) and null in the more baseline-saturated schizophrenia
presentations. Translated to the clinic, the eight archetypes sorted patients into
groups whose two-year functional-remission rate ranged from $14\%$ to $60\%$,
transdiagnostically (Figure~\ref{fig:prognosis}). The individual-level
discrimination gain was nonetheless small (functional-remission AUC $+0.017$
$[+0.009,+0.026]$ over a strong diagnosis-plus-severity reference): the map's value
is group-level prognostic stratification and continuous functional forecasting, not
a large boost to individual binary prediction. These results survived inverse-
probability weighting for attrition, restriction to reliably measured axes, and a
permutation null.

\subsection{It does not yet guide treatment---a boundary drawn by the evidence}

Finally, treatment data recovered from the per-cohort source dictionaries and
harmonized to drug-class exposures were run through a causal pipeline (common-support
overlap gate $\rightarrow$ propensity weighting $\rightarrow$ doubly-robust
errors-in-variables moderation with E-value sensitivity analysis) to ask whether
the durable axes \emph{moderate} treatment response. On observational
treatment-as-usual they did not reliably do so. Where identification was cleanest
(lithium in bipolar disorder, near-complete overlap) the result was a well-identified
null; an antipsychotic $\times$ metabolic/inflammatory signal for functioning in
bipolar disorder was suggestive but unconfirmed (average-effect E-value $1.79$); and
clozapine in schizophrenia was channelled to treatment-resistant patients and hence
non-estimable. Average treatment effects were uniformly confounding-fragile
(E-values $1.1$--$1.8$), the expected signature of confounding by indication
(Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:m5}). Importantly, the prognostic finding above was not merely
unmodelled treatment: the metabolic$\rightarrow$functioning forecast survived
adjustment for the drug classes patients were actually receiving (attenuation
$4.4\%$, credible interval still excluding zero). The boundary is therefore earned,
not assumed: demonstrating treatment \emph{selection} from this map would require
randomized or trial-arm data, which this baseline observational cohort lacks.
