%================================ ABSTRACT ====================================
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Psychiatric diagnoses group biologically heterogeneous patients, and dimensional
alternatives have struggled to show that any single biological signal is more than a
correlate of overall severity. We built \textbf{FACE-ATLAS}, a diagnosis-blind dimensional
map of $N=9{,}013$ patients with bipolar disorder, schizophrenia or major depression (three
harmonized FACE cohorts, 21 expert-centre sites), in which routinely-collected biology is
placed as a \emph{co-equal latent axis} alongside cognition, sleep and symptom dimensions
rather than as a downstream correlate. A single global Bayesian bifactor model---fit to each
patient's observed cells only (no imputation), with mixed likelihoods, uncertainty propagated
end-to-end, and the map frozen before any validation---yields eight axes: a general
functional-burden factor (\Gfac) and seven specific dimensions (cognition, immunometabolic
load, sleep, mania/activation, suicidality, developmental risk and substance use). The patient
space is a graded \textbf{continuum, not discrete biotypes} (apparent clustering is reproduced
by a single-Gaussian null), summarized by a five-archetype simplex that locates a patient's
clinical--biological state $\approx 9.7\times$ more tightly than the DSM-5 label. Allowing
\Gfac{} to correlate freely with the specific axes, one property recurs across every validation
stage: an \textbf{immunometabolic axis nearly independent of general burden} (sharing
$\approx 1\%$ of its variance with \Gfac{} versus $15$--$18\%$ for cognition and sleep---a
correlation of $\approx 0.10$ against $0.39$/$0.42$; robust to medication, adiposity and site), which is the \textbf{most temporally
durable} dimension (test--retest intraclass correlation $0.91$) and the \textbf{worst-prognosis
pole} for two-year functioning (functional remission $22\%$--$63\%$ across corners). This
separation is \emph{relative} and \emph{measured}, and converges with an independent
immunometabolic-depression literature. The map improves prediction of future functioning beyond
diagnosis and severity at the group level (held-out predictive-density gain $+62.8$) but not at
the individual level (AUC $+0.010$), positioning the immunometabolic axis as a
\textbf{cohort-enrichment and monitoring target rather than an individual treatment rule}. All
results are internal-validity findings on observational data; individual-level utility would
require external validation and incident-event outcomes. Placing routinely-collected biology
\emph{inside} the transdiagnostic factor space---as a co-equal latent axis rather than a
correlate of symptom dimensions---is, to our knowledge, the first such map across bipolar
disorder, schizophrenia and major depression.
\end{abstract}
