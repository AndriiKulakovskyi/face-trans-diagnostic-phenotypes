# FACE-ATLAS — External evidence base: how the findings sit in the literature

> **Map of record (read first).** The FACE map carries a **single immunometabolic biology factor** — one axis
> pooling cardiometabolic *and* inflammatory markers (BMI, HbA1c, lipids, CRP, leukocyte subsets) — within the
> **8-factor immunometabolic map** (G + 7 specifics; A = 5 archetypes). The external literature below uses
> "metabolic" and "inflammatory" in their own (separable) clinical senses; that is correct for the cited work.
> The FACE-side claim is that these markers load on **one** immunometabolic axis that is **largely independent
> of the general functional-burden factor** (the load-bearing biology⊥G finding). Canonical map findings:
> [`HORSESHOE_ESEM.md`](HORSESHOE_ESEM.md).

> An honest map of where the published literature **supports**, **qualifies**, or is **in tension with** the
> FACE-ATLAS claims, assembled to show the results are neither accidental nor isolated. Every cited paper was
> retrieved and verified in PubMed (title, authors, year, journal, PMID, DOI, abstract) before inclusion; papers
> that did not check out are listed in §8 rather than hidden. **According to PubMed** is the source for all
> biomedical citations below, and DOIs are linked as required.
>
> *Honesty principle:* the goal is an accurate evidence map, not a one-sided defence. Where the literature cuts
> against a claim, that is stated plainly — because reviewers will probe exactly those points, and because the
> FACE result is *more* persuasive when shown to survive the tension, not to ignore it. Compiled June 2026.

**One-line bottom line.** Every structural pillar of FACE-ATLAS has clear precedent; the continuum-vs-biotype
question is genuinely open (and FACE adds to a real debate rather than settling it); and the headline
"biology ⟂ severity" is corroborated *in direction* by a large, independent literature — provided the claim is
stated as **largely independent of a general clinical/functional-burden axis**, not as strict statistical
orthogonality.

## 1. The crux — "biological load is nearly independent of clinical severity"

This is the load-bearing, surprising claim, so it gets the most careful treatment. FACE-ATLAS reports the
**immunometabolic axis** is nearly uncorrelated with the general **functional-burden** factor `G` (cardiometabolic
markers ≈ 0.12, inflammatory markers ≈ 0.06 — both far below cognition ≈ 0.39 and sleep ≈ 0.42). The literature
does **not** provide a single paper that reports that exact contrast (a direct
within-sample correlation between a continuous biological-load factor and a continuous functional-burden
factor). What it provides is a large body of convergent indirect evidence. The honest reading splits into a
strong supporting case and a real tension that must be acknowledged.

### 1.1 Why the claim is well-grounded (support)

1. **Metabolic burden is driven by treatment, adiposity, age and chronicity — not by symptom severity.** The
   largest meta-analysis (52,678 patients) found metabolic-syndrome prevalence essentially **equal across
   schizophrenia, bipolar and major depression** (32.6%), with the only significant moderators being **older
   age and higher BMI**, and every antipsychotic raising risk versus drug-naïve patients (Vancampfort et al.,
   2015, *World Psychiatry*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.20252)). The diabetes companion (438,245
   patients) found multi-episode status — i.e. chronicity — the only significant predictor and only 2.9%
   diabetes in antipsychotic-naïve patients (Vancampfort et al., 2016, *World Psychiatry*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.20309)). A burden that tracks age, BMI and medication rather than
   diagnosis or symptom load is, by construction, weakly coupled to a severity axis.

2. **The biology is present before chronic severity accrues.** Antipsychotic-naïve, first-episode schizophrenia
   already shows impaired glucose homeostasis (fasting glucose g=0.20, post-load g=0.61, insulin g=0.41,
   HOMA-IR g=0.35; Pillinger et al., 2017, *JAMA Psychiatry*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2016.3803)); insulin resistance at age 18 predicts later
   psychotic experiences *before* clinical onset (Perry et al., 2019, *Schizophr Bull*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sby040)); and in 303 drug-naïve first-episode patients, full
   metabolic-syndrome prevalence was **no higher than controls** (Garrido-Torres et al., 2022, *Schizophr Res*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2022.07.007)). A partly trait-like, pre-severity biology is exactly
   what a low biology–severity correlation implies.

3. **Inflammation marks a subgroup and specific symptoms, not the severity continuum.** Only ~27% of depressed
   patients have low-grade inflammation by CRP>3 mg/L (Osimo et al., 2019, *Psychol Med*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291719001454)). Where severity is tested directly, inflammation tracks
   **mood state/episode but explicitly not symptom severity** in bipolar disorder (Fernandes et al., 2016,
   *Lancet Psychiatry*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(16)30370-4)), and **positive but not negative**
   symptoms in schizophrenia (Fernandes et al., 2016, *Mol Psychiatry*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2015.87)).

4. **There is an independently-developed framework that says biology is a separable dimension.** The
   **immuno-metabolic depression (IMD)** program holds that inflammation + metabolic abnormality cluster
   together in ~20–30% of cases and map onto *atypical, energy-related* symptoms rather than overall severity
   (Penninx et al., 2024, *Lancet Reg Health Eur*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101166);
   Milaneschi et al., 2020, *Biol Psychiatry*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2020.01.014); the
   melancholic-vs-atypical double dissociation in Lamers et al., 2013, *Mol Psychiatry*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2012.144), replicated longitudinally in Lamers et al., 2020, *Brain Behav
   Immun*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2020.04.002)). This is the closest external analogue to FACE's
   separable biology axes — arrived at by completely different methods, which is strong convergent validity.

### 1.2 Why "independent" overstates it (tension — state this in the paper)

1. **Case–control inflammation elevations are real and not trivial.** CRP–depression d=0.15 and IL-6 d=0.25
   (Howren et al., 2009, *Psychosom Med*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/PSY.0b013e3181907c1b)); larger in some
   meta-analyses (CRP g=0.71 across 5,166 patients; Osimo et al., 2020, *Brain Behav Immun*;
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2020.02.010)). These are *group* differences (ill vs. well), not
   severity gradients — but they show biology is not orthogonal to *being* ill.

2. **A modest severity/duration dose-response exists** for metabolic burden (Penninx & Lange, 2018, *Dialogues
   Clin Neurosci*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2018.20.1/bpenninx)), and greater symptom severity was
   associated with worse metabolic variability in first-episode patients (Pillinger et al., 2022, *Psychol
   Med*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291721005213)).

3. **Metabolic dysfunction does track a clinically meaningful outcome — cognition/functioning — in mood
   disorders** (Maksyutynska et al., 2024, *Psychol Med*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291724000345)).
   Because FACE's `G` *is* a functional-burden factor, this is the most directly relevant tension and should be
   addressed head-on.

4. **Small but non-zero genetic correlations** link CRP and depressive symptoms (rg 0.15–0.36; Kappelmann et
   al., 2021, *JAMA Psychiatry*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.3436)) — though that same
   Mendelian-randomization study reassigns most causal weight from CRP to **BMI**, which explains why raw
   inflammation–severity links attenuate after adiposity adjustment (also Zagaria et al., 2024, *J Affect
   Disord*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.072); BMI as mediator already in Howren 2009).

### 1.3 The honest synthesis (and a recommended rewording)

The cleanest, most defensible statement of the FACE result, fully consistent with the literature:

> *Metabolic and inflammatory load are **largely independent of a general clinical/functional-burden axis**:
> they are driven more by medication, adiposity, chronicity and subgroup membership than by where a patient
> sits on a global severity dimension, and they form a partly-separable biological axis — consistent with the
> independently-developed immuno-metabolic subtype literature. This is a relative, not absolute, independence:
> case-control inflammatory elevations and small severity/genetic associations exist, concentrated in a
> metabolically-defined minority and in specific (atypical/energy, positive-psychotic, suicidality) symptoms
> rather than in overall burden.*

Two points strengthen FACE's position when stated explicitly: (i) FACE's `G` is **functional burden /
impairment**, not symptom severity per se (Caspi's p is likewise read as impairment) — and biology being
decoupled from *impairment* while still real is exactly the IMD picture; (ii) the ≈ 0.07–0.12 values are at the
**low end** of what this literature would predict, so the paper should present them as a *quantification* of a
known qualitative pattern, with the boundary conditions (BMI confounding, subgroup concentration) named.

## 2. Transdiagnostic dimensional structure and the general factor

The dimensional/transdiagnostic premise is mainstream, not fringe. **According to PubMed:**

- **Insel et al. (2010)** — *Am J Psychiatry*. PMID 20595427; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2010.09091379). The NIMH RDoC charter: classify by dimensional constructs cutting across DSM. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Kotov et al. (2017)** — *J Abnorm Psychol*. PMID 28333488; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/abn0000258). HiTOP: an empirically-derived dimensional taxonomy built to fix DSM comorbidity/heterogeneity/instability. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Ruggero et al. (2019)** — *J Consult Clin Psychol*. PMID 31724426; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000452). Translates HiTOP to clinical use; candid that utility evidence is still accruing. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Caspi et al. (2014)** — *Clin Psychol Sci*. PMID 25360393; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702613497473). **The "p factor."** One General Psychopathology dimension fit better than internalizing/externalizing/thought; higher p = **more life impairment**, on a continuous/comorbid structure. *The closest conceptual ancestor of FACE's general functional-burden factor + continuum.* *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Caspi et al. (2020)** — *JAMA Netw Open*. PMID 32315069; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.3221). Four-decade follow-up: diagnoses shift while the general dimension persists — echoes FACE's "durable axis, moving symptoms." (Same Dunedin cohort as 2014, so a deepening, not independent replication.) *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Lahey et al. (2017)** — *Int J Methods Psychiatr Res*. PMID 28990308; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mpr.1593). A general+internalizing/externalizing bifactor fit best and was robust to dropping symptoms/sex — a strong psychometric defence of a real general factor. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Reise (2012)** — *Multivar Behav Res*. PMID 24049214; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00273171.2012.715555). "The Rediscovery of Bifactor Measurement Models" — the methodological lineage of FACE's bifactor/ESEM engine. *[CONTEXT]*

**The general factor's meaning is genuinely contested** — report this, don't bury it:

- **Heinrich et al. (2021)** — *Assessment*. PMID 34861784; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911211060298). Symmetrical bifactor models frequently yield anomalous loadings that change what the general factor *means*; advocate a bifactor-(S−1) reference approach. *[TENSION]*
- **Levin-Aspenson et al. (2020)** — *Assessment*. PMID 32909442; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191120954921). p fit well but its substantive interpretation varied across three large samples — no generalizable operationalization. *[TENSION]*
- **Watts et al. (2021)** — *Clin Psychol Sci*. PMID 36090949; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/21677026211055170). With multiple informants, p largely collapsed onto externalizing — mono-informant p partly reflects rater (method) variance. *[TENSION]*
- **Sun et al. (2025)** — *JAMA Psychiatry*. PMID 40560555; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.1258). In ~11,900 adolescents, a bifactor p mapped onto transdiagnostic polygenic risk and heritable brain-network topography — recent biological grounding. *[SUPPORTS]*

FACE's deliberately conservative reading of `G` as **functional burden with no symptom content** is, helpfully,
a partial answer to these critiques: by anchoring `G` only on functioning/severity items it sidesteps the
"what does p mean" instability that haunts symptom-only bifactor models.

## 3. Continuum vs. biotypes — the genuine counter-program

This is where FACE-ATLAS enters a live, unsettled debate, and intellectual honesty requires presenting the
strongest opposing evidence as such.

**Supporting a continuum / high heterogeneity (FACE's side):**

- **Wolfers et al. (2018)** — *JAMA Psychiatry*. PMID 30304337; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.2467). Normative modelling of brain structure in **schizophrenia and bipolar** (the FACE disorders): individual deviations were frequent but so heterogeneous that >2% patient overlap occurred at only a few loci — "the average patient is a noninformative construct." *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Marquand et al. (2016)** — *Biol Psychiatry*. PMID 26927419; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.12.023). Foundational normative-modelling method: map individual deviation from a norm instead of assuming homogeneous groups — the statistical embodiment of "continuum over categories" and a methodological cousin of FACE's uncertainty-propagated patient scoring. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Segal et al. (2023)** — *Nat Neurosci*. PMID 37580620; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01404-6). Across six disorders, person-specific deviations hit the same region in <7% of same-diagnosis patients, but aggregated into shared *circuits* in up to 56% — heterogeneity at the regional scale, partial convergence at the network scale. *[MIXED — useful nuance]*

**The biotype counter-evidence (the program FACE must engage):**

- **Clementz et al. (2016)** — *Am J Psychiatry*. PMID 26651391; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.14091200). The B-SNIP flagship: brain-based biomarkers in 711 psychosis cases yielded **three neurobiologically distinct biotypes that ignore DSM boundaries**, externally validated. Direct evidence *for* discrete biological subgroups. **Crucial nuance in FACE's favour:** the *same* data described DSM diagnoses themselves as "a single severity continuum" — i.e. categories are a continuum, but biology may still cluster. *[TENSION]*
- **Clementz et al. (2020)** — *Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging*. PMID 32600898; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2020.03.011). Biotypes have distinct, replicable clinical fingerprints. *[TENSION]*
- **Clementz, Tamminga, et al. (2024)** — *Adv Neurobiol*. PMID 39562461; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-69491-2_23). Recent synthesis: no neurobiological distinctiveness for DSM diagnoses (only a severity continuum), yet replicated Biotypes — and the authors now endorse **both categorical and dimensional** views. The fairest statement of the debate; cite it to show FACE engaged the strongest opponent. *[TENSION]*
- **Pan et al. (2026)** — *JAMA Psychiatry*. PMID 41739459; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.0001). Normative-modelling **plus clustering** still surfaced replicated ADHD "biotypes" — a methodological caution that a continuum conclusion is partly a function of analytic choices. *[TENSION]*

**How FACE should position itself:** not as proving "no biotypes exist," but as showing that *on its
8-factor clinical-biological coordinates across BP/SZ/MDD, a five-method structure gate finds no discrete
biotypes (ARI ≈ 0 vs DSM subtypes)* — a finding about *this* feature space, consistent with the normative-model
heterogeneity literature, while acknowledging that brain-biomarker spaces (B-SNIP) can yield clusters. The two
are not strictly contradictory: they interrogate different measurement spaces.

## 4. Metabolic burden vs. clinical severity (full citation set)

**According to PubMed**, ordered by stance. (Bottom line in §1; here is the verified evidence.)

- **Vancampfort et al. (2015)** — *World Psychiatry*. PMID 26407790; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.20252). N=52,678 SMI; MetS 32.6%; **no difference across SZ/BP/MDD**; moderators age + BMI; all antipsychotics raise risk. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Vancampfort et al. (2016)** — *World Psychiatry*. PMID 27265707; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.20309). N=438,245; T2DM 11.3% (2.9% antipsychotic-naïve); **multi-episode status the only predictor**. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Pillinger et al. (2017)** — *JAMA Psychiatry*. PMID 28097367; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2016.3803). Glucose dysregulation in antipsychotic-naïve first-episode schizophrenia. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Perry et al. (2019)** — *Schizophr Bull*. PMID 29635418; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sby040). Insulin resistance at 18 → later psychotic experiences (OR 2.32), before clinical onset. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Garrido-Torres et al. (2022)** — *Schizophr Res*. PMID 35878542; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2022.07.007). Drug-naïve FEP MetS (5.6%) ≈ controls (5.1%). *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Ohaeri & Akanji (2010)** — *Metab Syndr Relat Disord*. PMID 20964513; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/met.2010.0053). Metabolic liability "predates the era of antipsychotics" — partly intrinsic. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Moteshafi et al. (2012)** — *Drug Saf*. PMID 22967188; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03261978). Same drug (olanzapine), more weight gain in SZ than BP — phenotype/lifestyle, not severity. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **John et al. (2009)** — *Med J Aust*. PMID 19220180; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5694/j.1326-5377.2009.tb02342.x). MetS carried by BMI, not sociodemographic/severity markers. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **Donnelly/Perry/Khandaker (2022)** — *Psychoneuroendocrinology*. PMID 35286909; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2022.105707). Childhood metabolic factors → adult affective/negative symptoms via an immuno-metabolic bifactor — a developmentally early, partly severity-independent metabolic dimension. *[SUPPORTS]*
- **De Hert et al. (2018)** — *Dialogues Clin Neurosci*. PMID 29946209; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2018.20.1/mdehert). Shared-etiology (not severity-driven) cardiometabolic risk in SMI. *[CONTEXT]*
- **Ventriglio et al. (2014)** — *Eur Psychiatry*. PMID 24439513; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eurpsy.2013.11.005). Lipid change predicted by age/diagnosis/dose; severity (BPRS) link weak/counter-intuitive. *[CONTEXT]*
- **Adamowicz & Kucharska-Mazur (2020)** — *J Clin Med*. PMID 32079084; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020537). Within ill patients, MetS decoupled from measured behaviour/symptoms. *[CONTEXT]*
- **Penninx & Lange (2018)** — *Dialogues Clin Neurosci*. PMID 29946213; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2018.20.1/bpenninx). Notes a modest **dose-response** of MetS with severity/duration — the honest counterweight. *[TENSION]*
- **Pillinger et al. (2022)** — *Psychol Med*. PMID 37449481; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291721005213). Greater metabolic *variability* in FEP; severity and female sex associated with worse outcomes. *[TENSION]*
- **Maksyutynska et al. (2024)** — *Psychol Med*. PMID 38450447; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291724000345). Metabolic dysregulation → lower cognition in mood disorders. *[TENSION/CONTEXT — directly relevant since `G` is functional burden]*
- **Faugère et al. (2025)** — *Nutrients*. PMID 40218925; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17071167). **FondaMental/FACE-adjacent (Marseille, N=1,003):** vitamin-D/folate deficiency linked to *both* worse symptoms and higher MetS — a shared pathway that could couple the two. *[TENSION — and worth noting it is the same cohort family]*
- **Luckhoff et al. (2018)** — *Schizophr Res*. PMID 30503765; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2018.11.031). Antipsychotic-induced weight gain correlated with *greater* symptom improvement — the relationship is treatment-mediated, not a simple "sicker = more metabolic." *[TENSION/nuance]*

## 6. What is genuinely novel in FACE-ATLAS

Being honest about precedent also means being clear about the gap FACE fills. No retrieved work does all of the
following together, which is the defensible novelty claim:

- Prior transdiagnostic structure work is **symptom-only** (Caspi, Kotov) or **neuroimaging-only** (Wolfers,
  Marquand, B-SNIP). FACE puts **metabolic and inflammatory laboratory markers directly into the
  transdiagnostic factor space** alongside cognition, sleep, suicidality, mania and substance, across **three
  diagnostic cohorts (BP/SZ/MDD)** simultaneously.
- It **quantifies the entanglement** between each biological axis and a general functional-burden factor (the
  ≈ 0.07–0.12 correlations) — the immuno-metabolic literature asserts a *separable* dimension but, to our
  reading of the retrieved papers, does not report its correlation with a bifactor general factor.
- It does so with a **full-sample, no-imputation, mixed-likelihood Bayesian bifactor/ESEM** — a methods
  combination not seen in the retrieved transdiagnostic-biology papers, most of which subsample or use
  single-modality data.

So FACE is best framed as **the first quantitative measurement of how independent metabolic/inflammatory load
is from a transdiagnostic functional-burden axis** — converting a qualitative, independently-supported pattern
(immuno-metabolic subtypes; treatment/adiposity-driven metabolic burden) into a measured coordinate with
uncertainty. That is a genuine contribution *and* it is buttressed, not contradicted, by the prior literature.
